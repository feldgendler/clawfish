## M3.C (2026-04-29) — negamax alpha-beta core

First *searching* phase: replaces M3.A's depth-1 GreedyMover with full alpha-beta recursion at fixed depth (set via `go depth N`; default 4). Single-iteration; no iterative deepening, no qsearch (deferred to M3.D / M3.E). Calls `evaluate(pos)` at depth==0 (horizon effect accepted). Fail-soft negamax with `i32` scores, `MATE = 30000`, ply-adjusted mate-in-N, mate-distance pruning, triangular PV table (8 KiB inline on AlphaBetaMover), MVV-LVA capture ordering, M3.B repetition/50-move helper integration at `ply > 0`, 4096-node cancellation cadence with sentinel-return + post-call abort check + parallel root-score lockstep update. **ADR-0016** codifies the search structure.

### Strength match details (M3.D vs `baseline/material-greedy`, fixed depth 4, 20 games)

| Adjudication mode | Games |
|---|---|
| Resign threshold (`movecount=3 score=600`) | 13 |
| Mate detected (`Black mates`) | 7 |
| Total wins | 20 / 20 |

The 7 explicit-mate adjudications reflect qsearch's mate-detection-via-capture-extension working as designed: when alpha-beta pushes a tactical advantage toward mate-in-N, qsearch resolves the capture sequence at the leaf and propagates the `MATE - N` score, producing GUI-recognizable mate scores rather than just dominant centipawn evaluations.

### Why nps went UP, not down, with qsearch

Plan §6.2 anticipated qsearch would *lower* nps because qsearch leaves take longer per node. The opposite happened. Two reasons:

1. **Qsearch leaves are mostly stand-pat returns** at typical positions — captures+queen-promos are rare, the filter empties fast, the leaf returns `evaluate(pos)` directly. These leaves are CHEAPER per node than M3.C's `evaluate(pos)` at the same depth==0 frontier (which also called `evaluate` but at full negamax-frame cost incl. PV clear, mate-distance pruning, etc.). M3.D's negamax restructure (per plan §5) skips the empty per-frame work on leaves, delegating to qsearch which only increments nodes + polls cancellation.
2. **Total node count went DOWN** at depth 8 (~117M → ~84.7M). qsearch's tactical extensions allow the search to find mate-in-N earlier and prune entire subtrees via mate-distance pruning. The win in MD-pruning effectiveness more than offsets the extra leaf-work qsearch adds on captures.

Net: same milliseconds-per-decision, but more decisions per second AND more tactical accuracy per decision.

## M3.E — Iterative deepening + time management

### Headline numbers (Apple M4, single-threaded, release build)

| Metric | M3.D (single-pass) | M3.E (ID) | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Depth-8 cumulative nodes | n/a (single pass) | 72.45M | Sum across iterations 1–8 |
| Depth-8 iter-8 only | 84.7M | 63.22M (-25%) | qsearch + prior-PV hint together drop iter-8 cost |
| Depth-8 wallclock | 7.80s | **9.19s** (+18%) | M3.E pays for ID iterations 1–7 (cumulative ~9.2M extra nodes) |
| Depth-8 nps | 10.87 Mnps | **7.88 Mnps** (-27%) | Aggregate across all iterations; iter-8 alone in M3.E ≈ 7.62 Mnps |
| Depth-8 score | `cp 8` | `cp 8` | Same outcome |

The nps drop is expected ID overhead: the lower iterations are dominated by qsearch leaf-evaluation work (cheap PSQT + capture-extension), but their wallclock contribution accumulates. Per-node cost is similar to M3.D — `iter 8 nodes / iter 8 wallclock ≈ 63.22M / (9.19s - 0.91s) = 7.62 Mnps`, close to M3.D's 10.87 Mnps; the gap reflects qsearch's cumulative cancellation-poll cost stacked across more iterations. The trade is justified: ID's prior-PV ordering hint at iteration N (using N-1's bestmove) typically prunes more aggressively at the same final depth, AND the engine now plays at proper time controls (M3.D's single-pass `go depth 8` was a synthetic benchmark; M3.E plays `go wtime 5000` as production tournaments require).
