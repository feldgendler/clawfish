## M5.A (2026-05-01) — Null-move pruning

First M5 sub-phase. Adds a null-move sub-search to the negamax prologue: at non-PV interior nodes that pass a seven-condition gate, search a "passed turn" position to reduced depth (`R = 2 + depth/6`); on `null_score >= beta`, return a fail-high without entering the move loop.

Slots into the negamax prologue **after** the TT probe + cutoff check (ADR-0018 §13's pinned ordering) and **before** move generation. The first static-eval consumer in `negamax`; M5.B/RFP will share the read.

ADR-0023 codifies the formula (`R = 2 + depth/6`), gate set (seven conditions including `ply > 0` as defense-in-depth against future PVS refactor), zugzwang policy (`has_non_pawn_material ≥ 1`), mate-cap on cutoff, TT-store discipline (Lower bound at current depth with `best_move=0`, score = mate-capped value), and the deferred surface (verification search; eval-aware R bonus; threat extraction; bundling with M5.B/RFP — explicit "keep separate" decision).

**Fixed-depth bench vs fixed-time SPRT.** Bench is fixed-depth (`go depth 7`); the 66% node-count delta applies directly. SPRT is fixed-time (`tc=10+0.1`, etc.); under fixed-time NMP's node-reduction is partially recovered as +1–2 plies of additional depth at the same wallclock, so the SPRT Elo delta is NOT proportional to the bench-node delta. The two metrics measure different things; don't expect the bench-percentage to match the SPRT-Elo when comparing.

## M5.B (2026-05-02) — Reverse futility pruning

Second M5 sub-phase. Adds a purely static β-cutoff to the negamax prologue at new step 8 (between TT probe at step 7 and NMP at step 9): at non-PV shallow non-check non-mate-beta interior nodes when `static_eval − margin·depth ≥ β`, return `static_eval − margin` immediately without generating a single move.

No null move. No sub-search. No TT store. Cheaper than NMP by construction; ordered before NMP so an RFP cutoff skips NMP's null-search on the `d=3..6` depth overlap. ADR-0024 codifies the gate set, margin formula (`100 * depth` cp), return value (`static_eval - margin`), TT-store policy (none), lazy-dup design (own `static_eval` read inside own gate, not shared with NMP), and RFP-before-NMP ordering rationale.

A −37.2% result vs a −37.0% nominal ceiling is not a meaningful "outside" signal at the granularity of the underlying prediction model (the 1.5× conditioning-factor observation has ~10% spread itself).
- RFP fires extensively at depths 1–2 (the leaf/near-leaf population that dominates default-depth-7 bench), well within the 25%-upper-bound estimate before applying the 1.5× M4-ordering conditioning factor. The observed magnitude is consistent with literature priors.

## M5.D (2026-05-06) — Frontier futility pruning

Fourth M5 sub-phase. Adds **frontier futility pruning (FFP)** as a per-quiet-move skip inside the negamax move loop at shallow depth (`depth ≤ FFP_MAX_DEPTH = 2`): before recursing into a quiet move, if `static_eval + margin(depth) ≤ alpha`, skip it. The pruned move's proved fail-soft upper bound `static_eval + margin` is contributed to the node's running `best`, routed through `best_is_full_depth_after_score` with `move_is_full_depth = false` to suppress an inflated TT-Upper store from coarse static-comparison evidence (ADR-0026 §7 — load-bearing TT-store correctness commitment). Layered next to M5.C's reduce/skip decision in the per-quiet branch; FFP fires before LMR. At v1 constants (FFP_MAX_DEPTH = 2 < LMR_MIN_DEPTH = 3), FFP and LMR have non-overlapping depth ranges — pinned by a compile-time `const _: () = assert!(FFP_MAX_DEPTH < LMR_MIN_DEPTH)` invariant.

### Notable behavior shifts

- Bench nodes fell another **14.4%** relative to M5.C — modest compared to NMP/RFP/LMR's larger drops, consistent with FFP's narrow depth scope (only `d ∈ {1, 2}`) and per-move (rather than whole-node) granularity.
- No tree-shape pin in the existing test surface had to move. M5.A/B/C's depth-firings tests are at `d ≥ 3`; FFP fires only at `d ≤ 2`. The pruning ranges don't overlap.
- The provenance-downgrade test (`negamax_ffp_contribution_downgrades_best_is_full_depth`) caught a load-bearing correctness regression during the test-suite review loop: a fixture must structurally guarantee `pruned_bound > capture_score`, otherwise the downgrade arm is bypassed and the test passes vacuously. The final fixture uses a Q-for-P trade where the capture's qsearch return is ~−900 cp from White's perspective, while `pruned_bound = static_eval + 100 ≈ +1000` — ~1900 cp gap, structurally guaranteed.
- **SPRT outcome is the M5.D landing's most consequential finding.** The mixed-TC SPRT did not validate a positive strength delta over M5.C at the v1 parameters (`FFP_MAX_DEPTH = 2`, margin table 100/150/250). The aggregate is inconclusive (CI overlaps zero), but the per-TC pattern is strongly bimodal: positive at fast TC (10+0.1: 56.5%; 20+0.2: 68.3%) and negative at slow TC (40+0.4: 30.2%; 60+0.6: 40.6%) — opposite of every prior M5 phase's slow-TC amplification. Most credible interpretation: depth-2 ("extended futility") FFP is too aggressive at deep search, where its tactical-blindness risk grows faster than its node-savings benefit. Recommended follow-up: `FFP_MAX_DEPTH = 1` retune (frontier-only; Heinz 1998 original formulation). `baseline/m5d-ffp` NOT tagged at the landing commit — M5.E uses `baseline/m5c-lmr` reference until a future FFP retune validates positive.
