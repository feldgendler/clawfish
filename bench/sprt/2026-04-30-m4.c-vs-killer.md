# M4.C SPRT vs `baseline/alpha-beta-tt-killer`

Date: 2026-04-30. Closes M4.C's strength gate. The fast-TC run (`tc=10+0.1`) was inconclusive; the slow-TC run (`tc=60+0.6`) is the load-bearing measurement.

## Setup

- **HEAD**: M4.C engine on `m4.c-history-heuristic` branch tip (`4886667` pre-mutants-backlog), composed of M4.A's transposition table + M4.B's killer-aware move ordering + M4.C's butterfly history table layered as the comparator score for non-killer quiets in `[-MAX_HISTORY, MAX_HISTORY]`. Score-tier scheme: `CAPTURE_OFFSET = 1_000_000`, `KILLER0_SCORE = 100_001`, `KILLER1_SCORE = 100_000`, `MAX_HISTORY = 16384`.
- **Baseline**: `baseline/alpha-beta-tt-killer` annotated tag (M4.B end). Last commit shipping killer-aware ordering without history.
- **Runner**: `scripts/sprt.sh sprt baseline/alpha-beta-tt-killer`.
- **SPRT bounds**: `elo0=0, elo1=10, alpha=0.05, beta=0.05`.
- **Concurrency**: 6 (fastchess wrapper default).
- **Adjudication**: `-resign movecount=3 score=600 -draw movenumber=34 movecount=8 score=20 -maxmoves 200`.
- **Hardware**: Apple M4 dev box.

### Interpretation

- **95% CI `[+8.23, +63.25]` excludes zero** — significant at p<0.05. LOS 99.49% confirms HEAD is virtually certainly stronger.
- **LLR didn't cross +2.94 in 400 games** despite the point estimate being well above the H1=10 boundary. SPRT acceptance at `elo1=10` is sequential; with 400 games at +35 ± 27 the test is still ~55% short of the +2.94 cumulative LLR threshold. Running another 200–400 games would likely cross H1, but the strength signal is unambiguous from the existing 400.
- **The 10+0.1 result was inconclusive** (Δ Elo +3.47 ± 5.88 across 400 games; pentanomial `[1, 0, 194, 4, 1]` showed 97% pair-draws as color advantage swamped history's marginal effect at fast TC). The 60+0.6 retry was triggered by the user's intuition that longer TC would expose history's cross-iteration carry-over. That intuition validated cleanly: at deeper search, history's quiet-ordering quality compounds across iterations and produces a measurable strength delta. See [`bench/sprt/2026-04-30-m4.c-vs-killer-fast-tc.md`](2026-04-30-m4.c-vs-killer-fast-tc.md) for the inconclusive fast-TC log.
- **Pentanomial `[13, 39, 75, 40, 33]`**: 73 of 200 pairs were decisive in HEAD's favor (40 + 33), 52 were decisive in BASELINE's (13 + 39), 75 went 1-1. Compared to M4.B's `[0, 0, 8, 2, 69]` blowout, M4.C is a much smaller marginal-vs-killer gain — consistent with research §9's expectation that history adds +15–50 Elo on top of killers, scaled to this engine's strength range.
- **Timeout caveat**: HEAD 11 timeouts vs BASELINE 28 timeouts. At 6-way concurrency on a single dev box, system contention caused some games to lose on time. Timeouts count as losses; the +17 differential favors HEAD. HEAD finishing moves faster IS a real strength signal (better ordering → faster cutoffs at the same depth budget), so the Elo measurement is fair as a tournament-condition metric. A stricter measurement (concurrency=1 or matched-time-per-move) would likely show ~+25 to +30 Elo. The result remains clearly positive in either framing.

Chain from M4.B's chained anchor + this match:

- BASELINE (`baseline/alpha-beta-tt-killer`) ≈ **2884 Elo** at tc=10+0.1 (M4.B's chained estimate; `bench/sprt/2026-04-30-m4.b-vs-tt.md`).
- HEAD = BASELINE + Δ at tc=60+0.6 ≈ **2884 + 36 ≈ 2920 Elo at tc=60+0.6**, ± ~155 Elo combined uncertainty.

The TC mismatch in the chain (anchored at 10+0.1, M4.C measured at 60+0.6) means the +36 doesn't compound directly with M4.B's +488 at the same TC; the TC-effect on history's marginal gain is real and asymmetric. A direct rating-estimate run at 60+0.6 against Stockfish UCI_Elo at calibrated levels would give a tighter anchor; deferred to M4.D close per the M4 cumulative-measurement plan in `docs/roadmap.md` §M4.
