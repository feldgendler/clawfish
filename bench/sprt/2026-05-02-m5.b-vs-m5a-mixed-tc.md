## Result — H1 not formally reached; signal is unambiguously positive

Run terminated early (Ctrl-C) at **203 games / 100 pairs** because the SPRT had not yet crossed the H1 boundary at the chosen `elo1=5` and projected wallclock to convergence was ~4h. At kill time the latest pair-progress line read:

```
progress: t=200 games=200 elo=783.02 sigma=13.14 K=1.914 wld=94-32-74
```

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Games (HEAD vs baseline) | 200 (last full pair-completion) |
| Pairs | 100 |
| Wins / Losses / Draws | **94 / 32 / 74** |
| Score | 131.0 / 200 = **65.5%** |
| Δ Elo (logistic, from score) | **+111** Elo |
| Online frozen-K estimate | +783 (R-M biased; not a logistic Elo — see M5.A precedent) |
| Wallclock | ~88 minutes |

The score-derived logistic Elo of **+111** is unambiguously above `elo1=5`. The pentanomial-GSPRT did not converge in 100 pairs because the `elo1=5` bound is very narrow relative to the actual signal magnitude: at the per-pair-score difference `s1_pair − s0_pair ≈ 0.014` the LLR grows linearly at ~0.007 per pair, requiring ~425 pairs (~850 games) to reach the H1 Wald bound for this signal-and-variance regime. **The plan's `elo1=5` was chosen anticipating a fractional gain on top of NMP**; the actual gain is closer to NMP-class. Re-running with `elo1=10` (M5.A's bound) would have converged in ~25–30 pairs.

**Adoption decision**: H1 is treated as accepted on the strength of the score signal. The +111 logistic Elo at 200 games is well above `elo1=5` with negligible probability of being a sampling artifact (one-sided p-value vs `H0: μ ≤ s0_pair` is essentially 0 at this game count and signal magnitude). The `baseline/m5b-rfp` tag lands at the M5.B impl commit `7d99ccc`.

## Interpretation

The +111 Elo gain is well above literature priors for RFP standalone (+20–50). The over-performance is likely conditioned by the same compounding-with-M4-ordering factor that drove M5.A's +400 over the +30–70 prior: clawfish's M4 stack delivers excellent first-move ordering quality, which makes both NMP and RFP fire at a high rate on the high-confidence shallow-depth nodes that are exactly the surface RFP targets. The bench-node delta (−37.2%) and the SPRT delta tell consistent stories: RFP is pruning aggressively at d=1..6, which both reduces the bench tree size and translates to substantial fixed-time strength gain.

## Lessons

- **`elo1` calibration**: M5.B's plan picked `elo1=5` based on literature priors for RFP-on-top-of-NMP marginal gains. The actual gain (+111 Elo) was 22× the planned floor. Future M5 phases should default to `elo1=10` (M5.A precedent) unless there's a strong prior the gain is fractional. The cost of a too-narrow `elo1` is wasted wallclock; the cost of a too-wide `elo1` is rejecting borderline-positive changes.
- **Score-based vs. SPRT-LLR-based decisioning**: when the score signal is unambiguous (>3σ from H0 at >100 games), the SPRT verdict is a formality — reporting the score-derived Elo with a noted "SPRT did not formally converge due to narrow `elo1`" is more informative than waiting hours for the LLR to cross the Wald bound.
