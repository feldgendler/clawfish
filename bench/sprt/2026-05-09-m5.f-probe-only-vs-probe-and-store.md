# 2026-05-09 — M5.F probe-only vs probe-and-store mixed-TC SPRT (post-landing experiment)

Investigation SPRT triggered by M5.F vs `baseline/m5e-qsearch-correctness` returning `verdict=continue` with bimodal per-TC behavior (10+0.1 strong-positive, 60+0.6 slight-negative). Goal: discriminate which half of M5.F (probe vs store) is delivering the value, and whether a probe-only simplification would be a code-size win without strength loss.

## Hypothesis

Three possible outcomes:

| Outcome | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Δ Elo > 0 (probe-only wins) | Store side is dead weight or actively harmful — simplify to probe-only |
| Δ Elo ≈ 0 (statistically indistinguishable) | Store side contributes nothing measurable — simplify for code-size win |
| Δ Elo < 0 (probe-only loses) | Store side is load-bearing — keep M5.F as-is |

## Experiment shape

Branched off M5.F (`b92c815`) into `experiment/m5f-probe-only`. The only change vs M5.F: `qsearch_store_and_return` body replaced with a no-op pass-through:

```rust
fn qsearch_store_and_return(
    &mut self,
    _pos: &Position,
    score: i32,
    _bound: TtBound,
    _best_move: u16,
    _ply: u32,
) -> i32 {
    // EXPERIMENT branch `experiment/m5f-probe-only`: store side disabled.
    score
}
```

The probe at qsearch step 2.5 still runs unchanged; it just never finds anything stored from a prior qsearch call. (It can still hit entries stored by negamax, since negamax uses the same TT.)

## Interpretation

**The store side of M5.F is load-bearing by ~41 Elo.** Without entries to find, the probe alone is useless — and the per-frame probe overhead actively costs Elo.

The implication chain:

- M5.F (probe + store) vs M5.E (no qsearch TT): **+13 Elo** (mean of inconclusive `continue` verdict)
- M5.F (probe + store) vs probe-only: **+41 Elo**
- → probe-only vs M5.E: roughly **+13 − 41 = −28 Elo** (probe alone is a *regression* vs no qsearch TT at all)

Probe-only is strictly worse than both M5.F (probe + store) *and* M5.E (no probe, no store). Storing without probing was never on the table; probing without storing has now been ruled out empirically.

## Surprise: per-TC pattern *inverts* the M5.F-vs-M5.E pattern

M5.F vs M5.E was bimodal in the *opposite* direction: 10+0.1 was strong-positive (+9.8 percentage points), 60+0.6 was slight-negative (−3.5 pp). I had hypothesised the slow-TC slight-regression came from per-frame probe overhead eating Elo at slow searches.

This SPRT contradicts that hypothesis. The probe-only-vs-probe-and-store gap is **largest at fast TC** (32.3% — probe-only crushed at 10+0.1) and **smallest at slow TC** (48.2% at 20+0.2, ~46% at 40+0.4 and 60+0.6). Stored qsearch entries deliver the most value at fast TC, where the engine has less time to re-derive the same evaluation. At slow TC the engine has time to recompute; the savings shrink.

So the M5.F-vs-M5.E bimodal pattern (slow-TC slight-negative) has a different cause than per-probe overhead. Candidates:

1. **Random noise** — M5.F's slow-TC sample (60+0.6, 114 games) had Δ ≈ −24 Elo with sigma ~30 Elo per bucket; the slow-TC "slight regression" may simply be inside the noise floor for that sample size.
2. **Upper-bound looseness** — the no-Exact-in-non-terminal-qsearch rule (Stockfish 45e5e65) caps stored bounds at Upper for non-terminal paths; an Upper probe only cuts on `tt_score ≤ alpha`, which is a much weaker condition than an Exact cut. At slow TC, the search tree explores more PV-adjacent positions where the looser bound might miss a cut that an Exact would catch — but then the engine pays the recomputation cost. M5.F's slow-TC slight-negative would then be the price of correctness.
3. **Depth-0 entries crowding higher-depth ones** — at slow TC the TT fills more during a search; depth-0 qsearch entries compete for slots with higher-depth negamax entries. M4.A's depth-preferred replacement protects high-depth, but the age-bias term may still let qsearch entries displace stale-but-deep negamax data.

None of these are testable cheaply from this experiment alone. M5.G (singular extensions) and beyond will give us more data points on how qsearch TT entries interact with the rest of the search.

## Decision: keep M5.F as-is — do not simplify to probe-only

The store side carries the value. M5.F's full probe-and-store implementation is justified.

The slow-TC slight-regression in M5.F-vs-M5.E is left as an open question; future phases will surface more data on the underlying cause.
