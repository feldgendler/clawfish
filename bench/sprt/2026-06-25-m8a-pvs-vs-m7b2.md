## Reading

- **PVS is depth-amplifying, and the slow-TC win is real and replicated.** The
  60+0.6 bucket is clearly positive in *both* seeds (58.6% / 62.8%; combined
  70W-32L-82D = 60.3% ≈ +73 Elo). This is the strength-relevant TC (ELOH.D). The
  mechanism *works* where there is depth headroom to convert the scout-window node
  savings into extra plies.
- **But faster TCs regress, and the flat-weighted aggregate is net-negative.** At
  10+0.1 the prune-suppression cost of PVS dominates (combined ≈ −45 Elo); 20+0.2 and
  40+0.4 are also negative. Mechanistically consistent with the non-bench-identity
  insight (ADR-0043 §"Exactness"): the Step-3 full-window re-search flips
  `is_pv` false→true, which **suppresses NMP/RFP/FFP/LMR prunes inside the re-search
  subtree**. At shallow depth (fast TC) that extra work has no depth headroom to pay
  for itself; at deep depth (slow TC) the scout savings win the trade.
- This is the **mirror image of M7.B's inverse depth profile** (M7.B helped fast,
  hurt slow; PVS hurts fast, helps slow), and it is **exactly the situation
  M7.B.2's depth-conditioning was built to resolve.**

## Disposition

- **M8.A (unconditioned PVS): SHELVED — NO SHIP.** Production HEAD stays
  `M6.J` (eval) + `M7.B.2` (search). Code retained on branch `m8a-pvs` (not merged).
- **M8.A.1 (depth-conditioned PVS) is the deliverable, not a dead end.** The
  replicated +73 Elo slow-TC win is a strong prior. The M7.B→M7.B.2 playbook applies:
  condition the new-path cost on root depth so fast iterations stay cheap (≈ baseline)
  while deep iterations keep the slow-TC benefit. **CAUTION (M5.K lesson):** depth-gating
  an aspiration-width mechanism *backfired* once ("the adaptive benefit is not cleanly
  depth-localizable", −34.9 Elo) — so M8.A.1 needs its own careful design + SPRT, not a
  copy-paste of the M7.B.2 ramp. A smooth ramp with D0 sitting *above* the fast-TC
  median depth (the vindicated M7.B.2 shape) is the leading candidate. Decision deferred
  to the user; this is a new sub-milestone.
