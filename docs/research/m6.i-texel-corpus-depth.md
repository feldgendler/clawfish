# M6.I — Self-play search depth for Texel tuning corpus

Scope: choosing the search depth (or depth-ladder) used to play games for
the M6.I Texel-tuning corpus. The proximate question: at the engine's
current ~M6.F speed, our committed `depth_ladder=[(10,1),(10,1),(11,1),(12,1)]`
extrapolates to ~1-5 years to produce a 1-3M-position corpus on a single
10-core Apple Silicon host. Is shallower search defensible?

ADR-0003 honored: no engine source repos read; community-prose / wiki /
papers / forum threads only.

---

## 1. Executive summary

The literature does not specify a single "correct" depth. Published Texel
corpora use fast time-controls (often equivalent to depth 4-8 per move) or
fixed depth 9-12; the **defining signal is the game-result label, not
search accuracy**. Shallow self-play — including games played with only
material-value evaluation — has been demonstrated to produce useful tuning
signal. The practical floor is depth 4-6 per move, below which game
quality degrades enough that labels become unreliable. Switching from
depth 10-12 to depth 4-6 in clawfish's corpus pipeline is well-supported
by community practice. **Recommendation: depth-5 fixed, or a depth-4–6
ladder.**

---

## 2. What published tuners do

**Peter Osterlund (Texel — the method's inventor).** Original corpus:
64,000 games at `tc=1s+0.08s/move`, yielding ~8.8M positions
(~140/game). Not fixed-depth; TC implies mid-game depth roughly 5-8.
[Chess Programming Wiki — Texel's Tuning Method](https://www.chessprogramming.org/Texel%27s_Tuning_Method)

**Andrew Grant (Ethereal).** Hybrid: play fast-TC games at `1+0.01`
or `2+0.02`, sample 10 positions/game, then run a fixed depth-12 search
on each sampled position and apply its full PV — labeling the resulting
quiet position with the *original game result*. Datasets ~10M positions
("D12 resolved PVs"). The depth-12 search is a post-processing quiet-
extraction step, not the game-play depth.
[Ethereal HCE tuning data — TalkChess](https://talkchess.com/viewtopic.php?t=77502)

**Stockfish (NNUE — referenced for scale; not directly applicable).**
Uses fixed depth 9 as "the sweet spot" per the official training-dataset
wiki; later experiments moved to fixed-node (5,000 nodes/move).
Corpus scale: 16B positions for production nets. HCE is dramatically
smaller-parameter; this 9-deep / 16B-position regime is not the model
we should mimic.
[Stockfish NNUE training datasets](https://github.com/official-stockfish/nnue-pytorch/wiki/Training-datasets)

**Algerbrex (Blunder).** Used `tc=40/2+0.05` (ultra-bullet, depth ~4-6
equivalent) + quiet-position filtering. Mixed result: +34 Elo from the
tuned dataset, but some weights (mobility) converged to degenerate
values, suggesting label quality was imperfect.
[Blunder Texel tuning data experiments — TalkChess](https://talkchess.com/viewtopic.php?t=78536)

**Thomas Jahn (Leorik).** Bootstrap from a piece-point-value-only
evaluator (~700 Elo below peak Leorik). That weak version's games — which
must have been played with very inaccurate positional understanding —
were sufficient to bootstrap tuning, which then iterated for 6 generations
to surpass previous strength. **The clearest published evidence that
shallow / weak self-play works.** Specific depth number not in the
accessible devlog.
[Leorik devlog — TalkChess](https://talkchess.com/viewtopic.php?t=79049)

**Zurichess corpus.** 75k games between Zurichess versions; 20
positions/game; quiet filtered by qsearch margin; labels derived from
**Stockfish analysis** (not raw game results). The Stockfish-derived
label provenance is the exact ADR-0003 rejection reason in this project.
[Zurichess quiet generator — TalkChess](https://talkchess.com/viewtopic.php?t=71469)

## 3. Theoretical argument

**The label dominates depth.** The Texel loss is mean-squared error
between `sigmoid(score / K)` and the binary game result (0 / 0.5 / 1).
The label is the *outcome*, fixed regardless of how deep the game was
played. Shallower games produce noisier labels (more upsets), but do not
destroy the signal unless game quality collapses to random.

**Engine-strength modulation.** Whether shallow games give useful signal
depends on the playing engine's evaluation. A weak HCE engine at depth 4
makes more blunders than a strong engine at the same depth. Leorik's
bootstrap proves that *even a material-only evaluator* generates usable
signal — clawfish at M6.F (PeSTO PSTs + Tier-1 features) is substantially
above that floor.

**Quiet filter > depth.** Multiple sources emphasize that the quiet-
position filter (qsearch margin ≤ 25-30cp + opening-skip + score
clamp) matters more than the TC / depth at which games are played.
A chaotic position from a deep game is a *worse* training example than
a quiet position from a shallow game. Our pinned filter
(`QUIET_MARGIN_CP=30`, `OPENING_SKIP_PLIES=8`, `HIGH_SCORE_CP=600`,
`PER_GAME_CAP=10`) already implements this.

**No controlled depth-comparison study exists in the HCE literature.**
The claim "deeper is better" rests on community intuition, not
controlled experiments. The converse — shallow is sufficient — is
empirically supported (Leorik, Texel's own original corpus, Grant's
fast-TC games).

**The floor.** The one concrete published failure was Blunder at
`tc=2+0.02` — a weak engine + extreme time pressure. The implied
floor is ~5-10s total game time, or depth ~4-5/move equivalent for an
M6.F-class engine.

## 4. Fixed-depth vs fixed-time vs fixed-node

- **Fixed-time** — Texel's original choice; community default. Diverse
  positions, varied per-position depth. Hard to characterize precisely.
- **Fixed-depth** — clawfish's current choice. Reproducible, load-
  independent (matches our crash-safety + K-independence goals from
  M6.G). Equivalent to fixed-node for a single build.
- **Fixed-node** — modern Stockfish NNUE preference (adapts across
  hardware). For a single-build deterministic corpus, identical-in-effect
  to fixed-depth.

No published evidence of a quality difference between fixed-depth and
fixed-time corpora at similar per-position depth.

---

## 5. Corpus size

Practical published numbers:
- Texel original: ~8.8M positions; ~100 Elo gain.
- Zurichess: 725k — widely used despite "too small."
- Grant's HCE sets: ~10M each.
- NNUE minimum: 15M produces poor nets; 100M+ recommended.

**For HCE Texel: 1-3M is sufficient.** No published evidence that going
from 1M to 10M produces meaningful additional gain for HCE-class
parameter sets (~100-200 free parameters). Diminishing returns set in
fast.

---

## 6. Recommendation for clawfish

**Use a fixed-depth ladder in the 4-6 range.** Specifically:

- **Default for the M6.I production corpus**: depth-5 fixed (single rung),
  or the ladder `[(4,1),(5,1),(5,1),(6,1)]` if a small spread is desired.
- Retain the existing quiet filter (`QUIET_MARGIN_CP=30`,
  `OPENING_SKIP_PLIES=8`, `HIGH_SCORE_CP=600`, `PER_GAME_CAP=10`).
- Target corpus size: 1-3M positions (≈ 100-300k games at ~10
  records/game after filter + cap).

**Rationale:**
- Leorik bootstrapped from a weaker base. clawfish-M6.F is meaningfully
  stronger; depth 4-6 is comfortably above the noise floor.
- The published failure (Blunder) was a weaker engine + extreme TC;
  not our scenario.
- Quiet filter is already in place — the load-bearing quality gate.
- Throughput improvement: at depth 5, clawfish reaches iterative-
  deepening depth 5 on a typical position in ~2-10ms on Apple Silicon.
  ~1,000-5,000 games/hour with 10 workers vs ~5-15 at the current depth
  10-12. **100-1000× throughput improvement.** Target corpus in
  hours-to-days, not years.

**Calibration note.** Re-running `corpus calibrate-ladder` with shorter
buckets (e.g. `--buckets 25,50,100,200`) would pin a current-engine
ladder of roughly `(4,1),(5,1),(6,1),(7,1)`. Or skip calibration and pin
depth-5 fixed via a manifest override.

**Open question** (not resolvable without reading engine source): Leorik's
exact depth for its bootstrap self-play is not documented in accessible
forum pages. It's the closest analog to clawfish's situation; the
specific depth would be the most useful concrete data point. Current
recommendation is robust to that uncertainty — depth-5 sits comfortably
between "obviously too shallow" (random play) and "obviously deep
enough" (Grant's depth-12 PV resolution).
