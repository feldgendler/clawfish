# M8.A.1 — PVS Re-search × `is_pv`-Gated Pruning: Prior-Art Check

**Purpose.** After M8.A.1 shipped (depth-conditioned PVS, ADR-0044), the question arose:
the depth-conditioning ramp fixes a regression caused by PVS re-searches suppressing this
engine's `is_pv`-gated prunes — is that a *solved* problem with a standard fix, and is our
ramp idiomatic or a reinvention? This note records a focused prior-art pass on exactly that
interaction (distinct from [`m8.a-pvs.md`](m8.a-pvs.md), which covered the PVS/LMR *ladder*
mechanics but not this prune-interaction question).

**Provenance / confidence.** Synthesized from a chess-researcher prior-art pass (web: CPW,
TalkChess/OpenChess, NegaScout literature; **no engine source repos**, per ADR-0003). The
*verdicts* below are the researcher's; a few precise quote-level attributions are marked
**[verify]** where the exact wording should be re-checked against the linked page before being
treated as a direct quote. The structural conclusions are well-supported.

## The problem, precisely

This engine's NMP / RFP / FFP / LMR all fire **only at non-PV nodes** (`is_pv == false`), and
their depth/margin parameters were tuned **before PVS existed**. PVS marks the Step-3
full-window re-search node `is_pv = true` (the canonical convention — see Q2b), so those prunes
**switch off inside every re-search subtree**. Unconditioned PVS therefore regressed at fast TC
(prune-suppression cost with no depth headroom to repay it) while gaining at slow TC; M8.A.1
gates the scout by ID-root depth to engage PVS only where it pays (ADR-0044).

## Findings

### Q1 — Is the phenomenon documented?
**Yes for the mechanism; no for our depth-profile framing.** That forward pruning is suppressed
at PV nodes — **including re-search PV nodes** — is the universally documented standard. The
specific "fast-TC regresses / slow-TC gains" characterization of the *cost* is **not** in the
literature; it is our own framing of a known, depth-dependent effect.

### Q2 — Standard practice for avoiding/handling it

| Practice | Status in the literature | Bearing on us |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) Tune prune gates jointly with PVS active** | **The standard long-term resolution.** Modern engines tune NMP/RFP/LMR/futility with PVS already present, so the regression never arises. | Confirms our hypothesis: our pre-PVS margins are **composition artifacts**. The idiomatic fix is a joint prune-gate retune — not a PVS-side ramp. |
| **(b) Mark the re-search node NON-PV** (keep prunes firing) | **No published advocate found.** Departs from canonical node-type classification; CPW *Node Types* states re-search nodes **are** PV nodes. | A longer shot than "cheap obvious probe." Still cheap to SPRT, but unsupported by prior art. |
| **(c) Depth-condition the re-search / scout** (our approach) | One published cousin: **Reinefeld's NegaScout** proposed omitting the re-search at the **last two plies** (i.e. on *remaining* depth); CPW warns this **"won't work due to extensions"** **[verify]**. | Our conditioning is on **ID root depth**, not remaining depth, so it is **extension-immune** — it structurally sidesteps the exact failure mode of the only published depth-conditioning idea. **No published precedent at the root-depth level.** |
| **(d) PV / CUT / ALL node taxonomy** | The theoretical basis for *which* nodes prune, not a separate technique. | Background, not a fix. |

### Q3 — Is "PV vs non-PV re-search" itself debated?
**Not in the published literature.** The canonical position is that re-searches are PV nodes;
no dissent was found. (So option (b) is genuinely off the beaten path.)

### Q4 — Bottom line
**M8.A.1's ramp is non-standard-but-defensible.** There is **no cleaner *standard* solution**
short of a joint prune-gate SPSA retune campaign. It is *not* a reinvention of a clean
off-the-shelf method; it is a novel band-aid for skipping the idiomatic-but-expensive joint
tune — and a band-aid that happens to avoid the documented failure mode (extensions) of its one
published cousin.

## Recommendation

1. **Keep M8.A.1 as shipped.** The 2-seed SPRT (+28.7 Elo, rung-1) proves it, and prior art
   offers nothing better off the shelf.
2. **Backlog: joint prune-gate SPSA retune with PVS active** — the idiomatic medium-term fix,
   and the *same* lever needed for the post-NNUE re-tune (the M8.A.1 ramp constants are
   speed/depth-sensitive — see ADR-0044 / the NNUE-re-tune note). High-value follow-up; doing it
   could let us **drop the ramp entirely**.
3. **De-prioritize the non-PV re-search probe (option b).** Still a cheap SPRT if curious, but
   prior art gives it no support, so lower expected value than first assumed.
