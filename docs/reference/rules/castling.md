# Castling

*Source: Article 3.8 of the FIDE Laws of Chess (in force from 1 January 2023). See [`../README.md`](../README.md) for upstream source.*

---

## King Movement and Castling (Article 3.8)

**3.8** There are two different ways of moving the king:

**3.8.1** by moving to an adjoining square

> *[diagram: king movement — white king on e1 with open circles marking the squares it can move to, black pieces on surrounding squares showing captures]*

**3.8.2** by 'castling'. This is a move of the king and either rook of the same colour along the player's first rank, counting as a single move of the king and executed as follows: the king is transferred from its original square two squares towards the rook on its original square, then that rook is transferred to the square the king has just crossed.

> *[diagram: before white kingside castling — white king on e1, white rook on h1]*

> *[diagram: after white kingside castling — white king on g1, white rook on f1]*

> *[diagram: before black queenside castling — black king on e8, black rook on a8]*

> *[diagram: after black queenside castling — black king on c8, black rook on d8]*

> *[diagram: before white queenside castling — white king on e1, white rook on a1]*

> *[diagram: after white queenside castling — white king on c1, white rook on d1]*

> *[diagram: before black kingside castling — black king on e8, black rook on h8]*

> *[diagram: after black kingside castling — black king on g8, black rook on f8]*

## Castling Rights Lost (Article 3.8.2.1)

**3.8.2.1** The right to castle has been lost:

1. If the king has already moved, or
2. With a rook that has already moved.

---

## Castling Temporarily Prevented (Article 3.8.2.2)

**3.8.2.2** Castling is prevented temporarily:

3. If the square on which the king stands, or the square which it must cross, or the square which it is to occupy, is attacked by one or more of the opponent's pieces, or
4. If there is any piece between the king and the rook with which castling is to be effected.
