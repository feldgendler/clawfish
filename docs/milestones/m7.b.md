# M7.B — qsearch SEE-pruning

**Status:** ✓ SHIPPED 2026-06-16 (rung-1 by aggregate CI, user-approved "ship now, tune later"). Commit `011b0e2`. ADR-0040.

## What landed

The first **production consumer** of the M7.A SEE evaluator (`src/see.rs`, ADR-0039, landed inert). Inside `qsearch`'s main move loop, before `make_move`, skip move `mv` iff **all** of: not in check (`!in_chk`), non-promotion capture (`mv.is_capture() && !mv.is_promotion()`), and `see(pos, mv) < QS_SEE_PRUNE_THRESHOLD` (= `0`). One gate line + an allocation-free `qsearch_see_pruneable` helper whose `victim ≥ attacker` fast-out keeps `see()` off the hot path for winning/equal captures (only `attacker > victim` captures pay for the resolver). Promotions and (in check) evasions are exempt; the TT move is pruned on the same predicate (no exemption). The `src/see.rs` module-wide `#![allow(dead_code)]` was removed (`see()` now has a live caller).

**Δ Elo = +40.13 [+12.97, +67.80]** (pentanomial, 200 pairs, ptnml [12,42,64,52,30]; W-L-D 138-92-170). CI-lower +12.97 > 0 ⇒ **rung-1 ship-by-CI** (verdict=continue/llr=0.99 at the 400-game cap — the M6.I/M6.J continue-at-cap ship precedent). Largest single-change gain since M6.I.

### The caveat — inverse depth profile (slow-TC regression)

| TC | W-L-D (n) | score | ≈ Elo |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10+0.1 | 42-17-23 (82) | 65.2% | +109 |
| 20+0.2 | 45-23-38 (106) | 60.4% | +73 |
| 40+0.4 | 41-10-51 (102) | 65.2% | +109 |
| **60+0.6** | **10-42-58 (110)** | **35.5%** | **≈ −104** |

Pruning **dominates at fast/mid TC** (the −40% node count buys large extra depth when the budget is tight) but **clearly regresses at the slowest TC** (≈4.4σ on the W−L difference — not noise). This is the *inverse* of M6.I's depth-amplifying profile, and 60+0.6 is the TC most relevant to the eventual GM-strength goal. Hypothesised cause: `QS_SEE_PRUNE_THRESHOLD = 0` (prune *all* `see < 0` captures) is too aggressive at depth — at slow TC the engine has the budget to refute "statically losing" captures that are real tactical shots, so pruning them costs accuracy.

Shipped on the +40 aggregate (user decision "ship now, tune later"); the negative-threshold fix is **M7.B.1** (top of [`../tuning-backlog.md`](../tuning-backlog.md)).

## Lessons

- **SEE's Elo is in pruning, not ordering.** M7.A's good/bad capture-ordering split was flat (two variants, neither cleared a ship rung) because MVV-LVA+TT+killer+history ordering was already strong; M7.B's prune — same evaluator, different mechanism — landed +40. Removing work beats reordering it.
- **Mixed-TC can hide a per-TC regression of the opposite sign.** A clearly-positive aggregate (+40) carried a ≈−104 slow-TC regression. The per-TC breakdown (ELOH.D mandate) is what surfaced it; always read it, not just the aggregate. Here it motivated a concrete fast-follow (M7.B.1) rather than blocking the ship.
- **cargo-mutants TIMEOUT ≠ survivor when the mutated code is a speed optimization.** Under libtest's no-fail-fast single binary, removing a pruning optimization slows the whole suite past the prune-calibrated auto-timeout even though a killer test fails. Disambiguate by applying the mutation by hand and running only the killer.
- **Process scar:** `git checkout <file>` to revert a hand-applied mutation destroys uncommitted work in that file. Recovered byte-identically from a cargo-mutants worker copy; thereafter reverted mutations via surgical Edit / `cp` from a known-good copy, never `git checkout`.
