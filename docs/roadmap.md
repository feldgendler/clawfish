### M1 — Move generator + perft (standard chess)
Bitboards, all rules of standard chess, no search, no eval. Validated against perft fixtures generated by Stockfish 18 (sole oracle — see `decisions/0006-stockfish-as-perft-oracle.md`) on the canonical 6 test positions (starting position, Kiwipete, Position 3, Position 4, Position 5, Position 6) to depth 6 or 7. A larger EPD-format regression suite (also Stockfish-generated) is an optional bulk-confidence layer.

**Exit criteria:** perft suite passes; benchmark harness records nodes/sec for move generation. Target ≥100 Mnps single-threaded on Apple Silicon M4 (≥200 Mnps would be excellent).

**TDD applicability:** maximum. Perft gives exact integer answers from known positions.

**Status — prior-art research complete (2026-04-27).** Three parallel research reports in `docs/research/`: `m1-engine-architecture.md`, `m1-magic-bitboards.md`, `m1-perft-and-rust.md`. Synthesis recorded in `docs/prior-art.md`.

### M2 — Random-mover engine speaking UCI
Plays legal random moves through a tournament harness (fastchess; see ADR-0012 below). No search depth. Establishes the UCI skeleton, time-management harness, and tournament tooling before any search complexity.

**Exit criteria:** plays a complete game through `fastchess` against itself or another engine without protocol errors or illegal moves.

**TDD applicability:** very high. UCI is a text-in/text-out protocol — parsers and command dispatch are pure functions. Random move selection is deterministic given a seed. The end-to-end game loop is the only piece needing process-spawning integration tests.

**ADRs to write per-phase, as each binds.**

- **ADR-0011** — UCI I/O threading model. **Binds on M2.C.**
  - Constraint: UCI mandates stdin readable during search (`isready` → `readyok` mid-search; `stop` aborts `go infinite`).
  - Choice space: dedicated reader thread + cancellation channel to a search worker, vs. single thread polling stdin between search iterations.
  - Research: `research/m2-uci-threading.md` recommends reader thread + mpsc + per-`go` worker + `Arc<AtomicBool>` cancellation.
- **ADR-0012** — Tournament-harness conventions. **Binds on M2.E.**
  - Runner: `fastchess` (Cute Chess 1.4.0 ships zero macOS assets; fastchess ships pre-built `mac-arm64` binaries and is what Stockfish/Fishtest migrated to in 2024).
  - Engine config: `scripts/match.sh` wrapper. fastchess has no `engines.json` registry.
  - Output layout: raw PGN/log → `target/matches/` (gitignored). Milestone summaries → `bench/m2.md` per ADR-0010.
  - Smoke contract: 4 self-play + 4 vs Stockfish at `tc=10+0.1`, all legally terminated, no protocol errors, fastchess UCI-compliance checker silent.
  - SPRT (per-change strength gating): deferred to M3.
  - Research: `research/m2-tournament-harness.md`.

### M4 — Search basics
First efficiency layer over the M3 alpha-beta + qsearch + ID search. Cache search results across iterations (TT), stack good moves first (TT move, killer, history), tighten ID's outer-loop window (aspiration). Each piece individually SPRT-gated.

**M4 vs. M5 dividing line.** M4 is the *bookkeeping* layer — caches, ordering tables, window discipline. M5 is the *pruning / extension* layer — NMP, LMR, futility, singular extensions. The M5 techniques largely depend on having M4's infrastructure (TT-aware ordering, killer/history scores) already in place.

**Exit criteria.**

- Each phase commits its own SPRT-positive change vs. the prior phase's baseline tag, using `elo0=0, elo1=5` bounds at `tc=10+0.1` per `docs/workflow.md` §SPRT (or `elo0=-3, elo1=3` for marginal phases on a per-phase plan call).
- Cumulative M4-vs-M3 result measured at M4.D close as a fixed-game match (~200 games at `tc=10+0.1`) against `M3.F`, for rating-delta estimation. **Documentation, not a gate** — the per-phase SPRTs are the gates; M4 is closed when M4.D's SPRT accepts, regardless of the cumulative number. Investigation triggers: if cumulative is < 50 Elo, suspect a phase shipped an SPRT-positive change that doesn't compose cleanly with the others (typical cause: TT-move ordering interacting with history aging or aspiration re-searches); if cumulative is > 200 Elo (well above 4 × elo1=5 = 20 Elo floor), suspect either systematically conservative SPRT bounds or compounding interaction worth a research note before M5 planning.
- **Optional follow-up: mixed-TC cumulative match.** If ELOH.D has landed by the time the fast-TC cumulative match completes, run an additional overnight mixed-TC fixed-game match against `M3.F` to characterise the cumulative TC-amplification curve across M4. Recommended distribution: `--tc-sample 10+0.1:1,20+0.2:1,40+0.4:1,60+0.6:1` × ~600 games (~150 per TC). Reported in `bench/m4.md` alongside the fast-TC number. Documentation only; not a gate. Skipped if ELOH.D isn't ready by then — the fast-TC number stands on its own.
- `bench/m4.md` milestone summary per ADR-0010, recording deterministic `bench` node counts and SPRT / fixed-match deltas across A→D.

**Status — prior-art research pending.** To be written before M4.A planning begins (and refined per phase as each is planned). Existing `docs/research/m3-search-basics.md` and `docs/prior-art.md` cover the relevant background at the M3 level only; M4-specific design surface (TT layout, replacement schemes, history aging) is *not* yet researched. The parameter-level numbers in the sub-phase table below (window-widening schedule, killer slot count, history indexing) are tentative starting points from CPW and will be confirmed or revised by the per-phase research notes.

Acknowledged miss: a position visited as a qsearch *interior* node on Branch A (never stored under negamax-only rule) and reached as a horizon node on Branch B incurs a redundant qsearch on B; this is the +5–15 Elo gap between horizon-only and full qsearch-in-TT cited in the literature. Plan must specify how the search distinguishes PV from non-PV nodes (window-based: `beta - alpha == 1` is non-PV under PVS; M4.D's aspiration introduces the distinction explicitly), and tests must include a regression that constructs a position where TT-cutoff-at-PV would shorten the displayed `info pv` and asserts the PV stays full.

The plan must state whether the legality check runs unconditionally or only under partial-key, jointly with the key-discipline decision.
  - Open: per-node prologue ordering — MDP tighten (M3.C / ADR-0016) must run **before** the TT probe so the probe sees the narrowed (α, β) and more entries cut; mate-score correction on the stored score must run before comparing against the post-MDP window. Plan must specify the ordering as: repetition/50-move check → MDP tighten → TT probe (with stored-mate-score adjustment) → bound comparison vs post-MDP window → cutoff-or-fall-through. Tested with a position where MDP-first vs TT-first differ in cutoff rate.

**M4.A size rationale.** ~1000 LOC, exceeding the 300–800 typical-unit target. The phase couples TT data structure, probe-with-cutoffs, store, mate-score adjustment, `Hash` option, ordering integration, and `bench` adaptation. Splitting "plumbing then ordering" gives an artificial SPRT signal — TT cutoffs without TT-move ordering recover only a fraction of TT's Elo, so a "TT-cutoffs only" SPRT either fails (under-measuring) or passes weakly (giving the misleading impression that cutoffs alone are the value). Standard CPW guidance lands them together. The acknowledged size is the cost of a clean SPRT result.

**M4.D scope detail.**

- **Threshold gating.** Below a depth threshold (typical 4–6, picked in plan), search runs with full window — aspiration is a no-op at shallow iterations. Depth 1 trivially has no prior score; depths 2 to threshold-1 have a prior score but the score is too unstable to seed a narrow window.
- **Two-tier widening schedule with asymmetric fallback.** First try: window centered on the prior iteration's score with half-width ±50 cp — i.e. `(prev − 50, prev + 50)`. ±50 is the literature workhorse default (CPW range ±25 to ±100; ±50 is the middle-ground choice with the flattest expected-value curve across position types). On failure, **only the failed side widens** to use the fail-soft return as a tight bound:
  - Fail-high at `(prev − 50, prev + 50)` returns `L ≥ prev + 50`. Re-search window: `(L, +∞)` — keep the proved lower bound; widen the upper side to full.
  - Fail-low at `(prev − 50, prev + 50)` returns `L′ ≤ prev − 50`. Re-search window: `(−∞, L′)` — keep the proved upper bound; widen the lower side to full.

  No symmetric `(prev − 100, prev + 100)` re-search and no `(−∞, +∞)` fallback that re-searches the proved-empty range — both would waste work on the half of the score space already ruled out. **Two tiers chosen, not three**: a third intermediate tier is only profitable when its TT-primed cost is below ~`p · c_full_after_fail` ≈ 0.6 of a cold full search (sensitivity-analyzed during the M4.D walkthrough on 2026-04-30); the margin is too narrow to justify the extra plumbing and parameter-tuning surface in M4.D. Third-tier exploration deferred to M5 (see M5 row).
- **Care under fail-soft.** M3 returns fail-soft scores, so a prior-iteration score outside the prior aspiration window must trigger widening immediately on the next iteration rather than be re-used as a window center.
- **SPRT regression risk.** Aspiration loses Elo when first-move ordering quality is low (fluctuating scores → repeated re-searches). M4.D depends on M4.A–C delivering tight ordering; if M4.D's SPRT fails, suspect ordering quality before aspiration logic. Specifically: if the M4.A `Hash` default is unusually small for the SPRT corpus's middlegame positions, raise it (e.g. 64 MB or 128 MB) and re-run before debugging M4.D widening.
- **Mixed-TC SPRT + per-TC regression as the M4.D gate.** The M4.C empirical data (`tc=10+0.1` SPRT inconclusive at +3.47 ± 5.88; `tc=60+0.6` load-bearing at +35.74 ± 27.51) showed that fast-TC-only SPRT can be blind to M4-class changes whose Elo amplifies with depth. M4.D's gate replaces single-TC SPRT with a **mixed-TC SPRT** under the redefined game "draw TC from distribution D, then play standard chess at that TC." Implementation today: run 4 fixed-TC fastchess matches at `tc=10+0.1`, `tc=20+0.2`, `tc=40+0.4`, `tc=60+0.6` (~150 games each); the union of games is a discrete-uniform mixed-TC sample; apply SPRT `(elo0=0, elo1=5)` to the union for the clean accept/reject verdict. Separately, fit Δ(TC) over the per-(TC, outcome) tuples for the diagnostic curve shape. Both analyses run from the same data. Adoption rule:
  1. **Mixed-game SPRT accepts H1** (aggregate Δ ≥ +5 Elo with 95% CI), **and**
  2. **Per-TC Δ ≥ 0 at every sampled TC with 95% CI** (the "doesn't regress anywhere" floor).

  A width that fails (2) at fast TC but passes (1) is adoptable with a "helps deep searches but not fast" flag in the retrospective — same pattern M4.C followed. Wallclock per width: ~600 games × ~60s avg = ~10 hours unattended. Per-game TC sampling (true mixed-TC vs discrete approximation) deferred to the custom Elo-iteration harness in `docs/tooling-backlog.md`.
- **Window-width tune as part of M4.D close.** First-try half-width is parameter-tuned via the mixed-TC gate above, not deferred. Implementation lands with ±50 cp as the default (literature workhorse, flattest expected-value curve across position types). Then 2–3 pairwise width comparisons (±25, ±75, possibly ±100) using the same mixed-TC methodology against the M4.D-with-±50 build. Adopt the curve-winning width as the shipped default; that build tags `M4.D` at M4.D close. Total wallclock: ~10 hours × (1 primary + 3 width tunes) ≈ 40 hours of unattended SPRT runs, beyond the implementation LOC. Width tune is more impactful than adding a third tier — width changes affect every iteration's success rate; tier count (deferred to M5) only affects the failure path. **Depth threshold for activation** (typical 4–6) is also a tunable but is less sensitive than width; pick a single value (4 by default per CPW) at plan time and only revisit if the width-tune sweep is inconclusive.
- **Adaptive width deferred.** Width as a function of search depth or estimated time-per-move (e.g., tighter windows at deeper iterations where score volatility is lower) is a natural follow-up to fixed-width M4.D. Adding it to M4.D would double the parameter-tuning surface (base, scaling factor) and the SPRT campaign cost. Defer to a post-M4.D parameter-tuning phase, recorded alongside the ML-tuned aspiration item in `docs/tooling-backlog.md`. The fixed-width M4.D mixed-TC curve is the reference baseline that adaptive variants will be measured against.

A → B → C → D is sequential. B and C are conceptually independent each (separate quiet-move ordering terms layered on TT-move-first), but landing them sequentially is load-bearing for D's SPRT signal: aspiration windows benefit measurably more when first-move quality is high, and killers + history are the first-move-quality boosters for quiet-move positions. So the sequential order isolates each phase's SPRT cleanly *and* gives D a stable foundation.

**M6.A scope detail.**

- **Why tapered first.** Every downstream phase needs MG/EG separability to express asymmetric terms (passed-pawn rank bonus is EG-heavy; king safety is MG-heavy; mobility differs across phases). Tapering first means M6.B–E land *into* a representation that can already hold their terms. Tapering after one of them would require retroactive bookkeeping on already-shipped single-score parameters.
- **Bishop-pair + KBvKB-same-color + mop-up piggyback — and the per-piggyback STS sub-gates that defend the bundle.** Bundled because each individual term is too small to SPRT meaningfully on its own (bishop-pair ~5–10 LOC; KBvKB-same-color ~5 LOC of popcount; mop-up ~50–100 LOC of corner-attractor table + threshold gate). Tapered-foundation is the load-bearing change against which SPRT is calibrated; the piggybacked terms ride that signal. The bundle has an SPRT-signal-confound risk (a regression in one piggybacked term is hidden under tapering's larger Elo signal) — mitigated by **per-piggyback STS-theme sub-gates** in the M6.A retrospective: bishop-pair must lift STS theme #5 "Bishop vs Knight" by ≥ noise band (currently 73.7% so a +1.5% absolute = ~+15 credit non-regression floor); mop-up must lift STS theme #11 "King Activity" by ≥ noise band (currently 42.3% so a non-trivial lift is expected, +30+ credit); KBvKB-same-color has no theme coverage so guarded by unit-tested correctness only (FEN-parsed KBvKB-light + KBvKB-dark positions both `evaluate` to 0). A piggyback that *regresses* its theme group is a `should-fix` even if the overall SPRT accepts — same discipline as M5.E's "correctness over Elo" precedent.
- **`Position` field migration.** `static_eval_white: i32` becomes either a triple `(static_mg_white, static_eg_white, phase): (i32, i32, u8)` or a pair `(static_eval_packed: i64, phase: u8)`. The `pub(crate) fn static_eval_white(&self) -> i32` accessor at `src/position.rs:504` is replaced by an `evaluate(&Position)` consumer that does the blend; intermediate accessors (`static_mg_white`, `static_eg_white`, `phase`) are added for the incremental-delta path. `refresh_static_eval` / `refresh_static_eval_from` extended to all three fields.
- **Bench expectation, honestly.** Per-call eval cost rises ~1.5× (two PSQT lookups + phase mix). Tree shape is preserved (no new pruning) but **node count *will* change** at fixed depth: a different leaf score changes root-move ordering, which changes node count even with identical search code. The M5.H1 "bench is the no-regression gate" property is consumed at M6.A — M6.A's bench number becomes the new HEAD anchor, M6.B's SPRT runs against that, etc. NPS will drop modestly; SPRT Elo gain (EG king PSTs and bishop-pair on the bench corpus) is expected to absorb the cost.
- **Round-trip assert cost under tapering.** The always-on debug-build round-trip assert in `make_move`/`unmake_move` (C1–C4 tests at `src/mov.rs:~3141+`) becomes either three asserts per make/unmake (triple form) or one assert on the packed-i64 + one on `phase` (packed form). The triple-form increase may push C4's proptest budget over the default timeout; M6.A's test plan includes a timing-sanity-check on C4 and adjusts proptest `cases` if necessary.

**M6.B scope detail.**

- **Pawn hash necessity.** Pawn-structure terms get computed on every eval call; pawn-position stability across consecutive evals in the search tree is high (~70–90%), so hash-caching is the standard accelerator (CPW reports 10–15× speedup on pawn-term cost). Skipping the hash punts the cost into per-leaf eval, which then compounds with M6.D (mobility has no caching) and M6.E (king-safety reads pawn-shield from the same place).
- **Pawn Zobrist substream choice.** Two options weighed in the ADR. (i) Reuse the M1.D / ADR-0009 pawn entries of the 781-key set with masked accumulation. (ii) Maintain a sibling pawn-only key set XOR-tracked across the twelve relevant flag arms (see M6.B ADR-bullets). Option (ii) is the literature default for cache locality and update sparseness; option (i) saves keys but adds masked-XOR cost on every move.
- **Test discipline for the pawn-substream update.** Named regression tests required for: (a) `Quiet`-pawn-move pawn-key round-trip; (b) `DoublePush` pawn-key round-trip; (c) pawn-`Capture`-of-pawn (both pawns leave the board on different squares — the simpler analogue of EP); (d) pawn-`Capture`-of-non-pawn; (e) non-pawn-`Capture`-of-pawn; (f) **`EnPassant`** — the two-pawn-square-delta case, with `capture_sq ≠ to`; (g) all four `*Promo` arms (pawn out at `from`, no pawn in at `to`); (h) **all four `*Promo`-with-capture arms** (pawn out at `from`, captured piece out at `to`, promoted piece in at `to`; if captured piece was a pawn, the substream sees a *second* pawn removal at `to`). Each test does a make/unmake round-trip and asserts the pawn-Zobrist returns to its prior value (analogous to the M1.E Zobrist round-trip discipline). Families (g) and (h) each cover all four promotion-kind variants as a single test parameterized over `MoveFlag::KnightPromo` / `BishopPromo` / `RookPromo` / `QueenPromo`; the pawn-substream delta is structurally identical across the four kinds, so the count gap (12 enumerated flag arms vs. 8 test families) is intentional, not an undercount.

**M6.D scope detail.**

- **Pinned pieces are scored pseudolegally — a deliberate deferral, not an oversight.** The committed term counts a pinned piece's *full* pseudolegal attack set (∩ mobility area); an absolutely-pinned knight (0 legal moves) or a file/rank-pinned bishop (0 legal moves) is credited as if mobile. Rationale: (i) eval is called without movegen context (qsearch / static eval do not enumerate moves), so pin-awareness would add king-ray pin detection to every eval leaf — a new cost on the hottest path; (ii) pins are transient tactics the search resolves within a few plies, so pricing them in eval risks double-counting against search; (iii) the M6.I joint Texel pass absorbs the average over-credit into a marginally lower per-count weight (the M6.B/C "re-derive shapes against our PeSTO PSTs, don't trust literature raw" lesson). Same deferral *class* as the row's "richer minor-piece-X-ray exclusion deferred" — a correctness layer on top of the pseudolegal base, sized out of the ~400 LOC unit on purpose. The engine is not pin-blind globally: legal movegen filters pinned moves (perft-exact) and search sees pins tactically; only the mobility *eval term* is pin-agnostic. Revisit only if a post-M6 SPRT diagnostic attributes measurable loss to pinned-piece mobility over-credit.

**M6.E scope detail.**

- **Why last among A–E.** King safety is the noisiest term to SPRT (Elo varies materially by SPRT opening pool — kingside-attacking pools amplify, positional pools dilute). Landing it after pawn structure (M6.B) is a *real code dependency*: king safety reads `pawn_shield_files[color]` from the pawn hash without re-evaluating pawn structure. Landing it after mobility (M6.D) is *not* a code dependency (the per-piece-kind attack bitboards are already in the codebase since M1; M6.D doesn't introduce them, it just reads them for a new term). E-after-D is for **SPRT-signal cleanliness**: king-safety's attacker-count interacts with mobility's mobility-area exclusion (an enemy-pawn-attacked square that excludes from mobility area is *also* a candidate king-zone attack square), and landing them in either order moves which phase's SPRT signal captures the interaction. E-after-D fixes the attribution.
- **Tapering policy.** King safety is traditionally MG-heavy or MG-only (king becomes an attacker in the EG). ADR considers full-tapered with EG weights near zero vs. hard-off in EG; CPW evidence mildly favors full-tapered for representational simplicity.

**M6.F scope detail.**

- **Three Tier-1 HCE features, added before the joint Texel pass.** Binding research: `docs/research/m6-remaining-hce-features.md` (Priority-1 list). (1) **Outposts** — a knight or bishop in the enemy half, on a square no enemy pawn can ever attack (the pawn-attack front-span / hole predicate), defended by an own pawn; per-square rank-scaled bonus. Reads M6.B's `pawn_attack_spans` fills + the `file_fill` helper already present — a *real code dependency on M6.E* (the M6.B pawn-data infrastructure must be in tree). The structural discriminator that mobility/PST cannot make: pawn-supported-and-unchallengeable vs. merely-central placement. (2) **Rook on open/semi-open file** — `file_fill(rooks) & ~(file_fill(own_pawns) | file_fill(enemy_pawns))` for open, semi-open the own-pawn-absent variant; mobility counts moves but not file-open status. (3) **Endgame scaling / draw-dampening** — a multiplicative reduction on the blended eval for structurally drawish material: opposite-colored-bishops-with-pawns, pawnless-draw patterns (KNN vs K etc.), and a 50-move-proximity taper. **KBvKB-same-color already exists in M6.A (`is_insufficient_material`) — do not duplicate it**; the missing cases are OCB-with-pawns + the material-only pawnless patterns + the move-counter taper.
- **Disposition: score-neutral inert landing, per the established M6.C/M6.D/M6.E precedent.** All new weights zeroed ⇒ outpost/rook-file terms ≡ (0,0), endgame-scaling multiplier ≡ identity (1.0) ⇒ `evaluate` byte-identical to `M6.E` ⇒ bench `1213649`/d4 `90591` byte-for-byte ⇒ provably inert ⇒ no confirmation SPRT, no SPRT screen ladder. `*_IN_EVAL=true`-style flags + term math live at zero weight, M6.I-ready (the M6.B–E `*_IN_EVAL` precedent). No Elo claim ⇒ roadmap line-296 satisfied vacuously (the M6.C/M6.D/M6.E inert-landing disposition).
- **Open ADR question — endgame-scaling may land live (record, do not pre-decide).** The research classes endgame-scaling as a *correctness* feature (draw recognition is a structural search-misguidance fix, not an Elo-chasing weight) that "does not need Texel calibration for the scaling coefficient" (literature OCB multiplier ≈ 0.5–0.75). The M6.F plan/ADR *may* therefore elect to land that one sub-feature **live** behind a correctness gate (the M5.E "correctness over Elo" precedent — landed inconclusive-Elo because correctness was the gate), with outposts + rook-file still inert pending M6.I. **Secondary argument — optimizer tractability (recorded 2026-05-19; reinforces *live*, does not decide it).** The entire A–F additive surface is *linear in the weight vector* — the MG/EG taper preserves this (`phase` is position data, not a parameter) — which is exactly what makes M6.I's Texel feature-caching + closed-form gradient tractable. Endgame-scaling is the **only parameter-bearing multiplicative construct in all of M6**. If its coefficient is a Texel tunable, the per-position score `s_i = scale(θ)·(w·f_i)` is **bilinear in (θ, w)**: it breaks per-position feature caching for the θ-gradient, opens the `K·scale` gauge ridge the M6.I optimizer-stopping sub-protocol *already* flags, and the structural/gate parts (pattern set, 50-move threshold) have ≈zero gradient a.e. Landing it **live behind the correctness gate with a fixed literature coefficient (≈0.5–0.75)** keeps it *out* of the joint Texel surface: `scale_i` becomes a known per-position constant that folds into the cached feature mask (`g_i = scale_i·f_i`), preserving full linearity-in-weights and the closed-form gradient. So optimizer tractability **independently reinforces** the correctness case for *live* (or, at minimum, for keeping the coefficient fixed and out of M6.I). **Caveat preserving the open question:** this bites only the gradient / finite-difference Texel paths — an **SPSA** optimizer (the M6.I optimizer ADR is still open) is gradient-free and indifferent to the multiplier, so the argument is *conditional on the M6.I optimizer choice*, not absolute. This is recorded here as an **open question**; the default framing is inert-per-precedent (all three zeroed) unless the M6.F ADR argues the correctness case. Either way the outpost + rook-file weights and (if inert) the endgame-scaling coefficient enter the M6.I joint Texel pass.
- **Sequencing.** After M6.E (real code dependency — outpost/rook-file reuse the M6.B pawn-attack-span fills + `file_fill` helper, which must be in tree); before M6.G/M6.I (the "extend then tune" law — Texel cannot discover absent features, so they must exist before the one joint pass; adding them after M6.I would force a second Texel pass). Computed live in `evaluate_core` (the ADR-0032 §3 / ADR-0033 §6 live-term class).
- **Size / Elo.** ~110–150 LOC total (uses infrastructure already present — M6.B pawn fills, `file_fill`). Expected Elo n/a at M6.F (score-neutral; weights → M6.I). Literature priors recorded as the M6.I expectation: outposts ~20–30 (incl. the empirically-measured STS theme #3 "Knight Outposts" −40 gap on the M6.D rejected-config build — the load-bearing justification, a confirmed not merely-theoretical blindspot), rook-file ~15–25, endgame-scaling ~10–15.

**M6.G scope detail.**

- **Why corpus is its own sub-milestone.** The labeled-position corpus is reusable foundational infrastructure with distinct *data* failure modes (label-provenance leakage, decisive/draw imbalance, FEN duplication, held-out-split contamination, non-reproducible acquisition). It is consumed by **M6.I** (the Texel pass), the tuning-backlog **"PST co-tuning" Arm B**, **future SPSA tuning-backlog campaigns**, and **M11 NNUE data-prep** — burying it inside the M6.I tuning pass would re-derive it for each consumer and hide its data gate. It therefore gets its own data-quality gate, not an SPRT.
- **Source mixture + corpus discipline.** Labeled position corpus is *public chess data* per CLAUDE.md ground rules + ADR-0003 — vendored as a data file in `bench/`, restricted to **game-result labels** (PGN game outcome; EPD with `c9 "result"` outcome). Sources: CCRL PGN snapshots + band-filtered Lichess open-database PGNs + diversified-opening-book clawfish self-play + the (label-verified) Zurichess quiet set. Engine-score-labeled corpora are out of scope (would leak the labeling engine's eval bias into our weights — an ADR-0003 concern by spirit); the M6.G ADR runs an **ADR-0003 label-provenance audit** that must *verify* the Zurichess labels are game outcomes, not engine scores. See the corpus-construction ADR-bullet list above. The Texel-original 8.5M-position set is *not* in scope without an explicit ADR-0003 amendment.
- **Filtering pipeline.** Clock-loss / time-forfeit exclusion + TC-class filter; opening-ply skip; FEN dedup + per-FEN caps; quiet-position extraction. The quiet *definition* is fixed by **M6.I's tuner qsearch** — an M6.G↔M6.I interface-contract item pinned at M6.G plan time (interface-first), not chosen freely at M6.G.
- **Corpus-mixture division of labor (the M6.G↔M6.I interface contract).** M6.G delivers the *sources*, the held-out **self-play, deployment-distributed validation set**, the *stratified* selection objective (aggregate held-out logistic loss **plus** per-theme STS blind-spot strata — reuse the M6.A per-theme STS sub-gate discipline; outposts = STS theme #3), and the selection harness. The *actual mixture selection* (bi-level: inner Texel per candidate, outer coarse simplex search, **K refit per candidate**, SPRT only to confirm the winner) runs in **M6.I** because it needs the inner Texel. **TC-regime mix is a *nested* meta-axis** — a sub-simplex inside the self-play source (TC is *generated*, hence controllable, for self-play; only *filterable* for the external sources), **prior-pinned to the deployment mixed-TC SPRT profile (R-TC)** with at most a narrow searched residual, not a co-equal free dimension. **Precondition (an M6.G deliverable constraint, not an M6.I choice):** the held-out objective must itself be TC-stratified to the deployment profile, else the TC-mix axis has no honest selection signal and falls back to the deployment-mirror prior by fiat.
- **Reproducibility.** Harness outputs a frozen corpus snapshot + manifest + RNG seeds (acquisition + corpus shuffle) + a re-run script that reproduces the frozen corpus, all vendored in `bench/`.
- **Gate: data-quality checks, NOT SPRT.** The M5.E correctness-only-gate precedent applied to *data*: coverage stats, decisive/draw balance, dedup ratios, label-provenance audit pass, a reproducibility re-run match, held-out-split integrity. No Elo claim, no SPRT (corpus is data infra). M6.G's "tag" is a **frozen corpus-artifact + manifest reference, not an engine-build SPRT baseline** (see the "Baseline tags expected" table — M6.I's SPRT baseline remains the `M6.F` features tag).
- **Sequencing / parallelization.** Order is M6.F (features) → M6.G (corpus infra) → M6.H (on-demand ingestion) → M6.I (tuning). Corpus acquisition/filtering infra (M6.G) is largely independent of the M6.F eval features and **can proceed in parallel with M6.F**; but the quiet-position definition + held-out set + stratified objective are pinned by the M6.I tuner contract at M6.G plan time (interface-first). M6.H extends M6.G's `pgn-shard.bin` write path with a network-layer fetcher; M6.H plan/tests can run in parallel with M6.G implementation as long as the `Source` taxonomy + the `corpus ingest-pgn` shape are already pinned. M6.I needs the M6.G corpus + the M6.H fetcher (its bi-level driver invokes both as primitives), so M6.I is strictly after both.

**M6.H scope detail — robust on-demand Lichess/CCRL ingestion.**

M6.H is the **data-acquisition layer** that turns the M6.G corpus infra into a self-replenishing input pipeline for M6.I's bi-level tuner: the M6.I outer loop asks for "N more positions from source X" and M6.H delivers them, blocking the campaign on network failures until the data arrives. Without it, M6.I's learning-curve experiment (the meta-optimization over N, source mix, etc.) would require pre-staged multi-GB downloads ahead of time; with it, the operator runs `corpus selfplay --opening-mode={book,random}` and `corpus fetch --source={ccrl,lichess}` on demand inside the campaign.

- **Pure-Rust HTTP + decompression — no system-tool deps for the fetcher.** New deps: `ureq` (sync HTTP client; TLS via rustls validating against the OS trust store — landed via the `platform-verifier` feature, which subsumes `rustls-native-certs`; HTTP Range headers, per-read timeouts), `zstd` (streaming Zstandard decoder for Lichess monthly `.pgn.zst` dumps), `zip` (ZIP reader). **Amendment (landed 2026-05-22): `.7z` IS supported via `sevenz-rust2` (pure-Rust, Apache-2.0, LZMA/LZMA2) — the original "7z out of scope" was wrong twice over: CCRL distributes ONLY as `.7z`, and a good native-Rust 7z decoder exists.** All crates are general-purpose, so ADR-0003 isn't in the way. See ADR-0036 (+ 7z amendment).
- **Streaming + early termination — don't download more than necessary.** The pipeline is `HTTP → decompress → stream_pgn → filter → ingest into pgn-shard.bin`, with the request driven by the M6.I outer loop's `positions_ingested >= target` check. When the target is hit, dropping the reader closes the connection cleanly; for a 250K-position request against a Lichess monthly dump, that means downloading ~5–10% of the full file, not the whole thing.
- **In-memory resume via `ResumableHttpReader<R: Read>`.** Wrap the `ureq` body reader in a custom `Read` impl that tracks `bytes_received` and, on transient error (TCP RST, stall detection, timeout), backs off + reopens the request with `Range: bytes={bytes_received}-`, then resumes feeding bytes into the same `zstd::Decoder` instance. Decoder state stays in RAM across retries → zero re-decompression cost on a network blip. The caller (`zstd::Decoder` → `stream_pgn`) never knows a retry happened.
- **Stall = no progress, not no bytes (mechanism + policy, two layers).** A stall is defined as *"no new PGN game parsed for `T_stall` (≈60–90s)"* — progress toward the goal, not raw transport liveness. This catches the connection that dribbles bytes too slowly to ever yield a game, and the one that delivers bytes which decompress but never parse — both invisible to a byte-level read timeout. **Mechanism:** `TcpStream::set_read_timeout` is set *short* (~10s) purely as a wake/heartbeat — a `read()` timeout is **not** itself a failure; it just unblocks the thread to re-check (a) the shutdown signal and (b) the stall watchdog, then loops and reads again (a truly dead socket would otherwise block the syscall forever and the watchdog could never run). **Policy:** maintain `last_game_at`, bumped each time `stream_pgn` yields a game; initialize it to attempt-start so stream warm-up (TCP connect + zstd frame header before the first complete game emerges) gets the full `T_stall` window; abort the attempt when `now − last_game_at > T_stall`. **Signal is games parsed, not positions ingested** — the filter legitimately rejects most games (opening plies, non-quiet positions), so keying on ingested positions would false-positive on a healthy-but-selective stream. A stall aborts → resume via `Range:` (above). **Escalation:** a stall that *immediately re-stalls* after resume (bounded count of consecutive resume-then-stall) escalates to discard-and-restart-from-zero rather than looping forever on a byte-alive-but-semantically-dead stream. Total elapsed time is not capped; the outer infinite-backoff loop handles long absences.
- **Outer infinite-backoff loop — "wait until network resolves."** On each unrecoverable attempt failure: sleep with exponential backoff (1s, 2s, 4s, …, capped at 5 min), then retry. Honors SIGINT for clean exit. ETAs logged in absolute local clock time per CLAUDE.md ground rules.
- **Two distinct defenses: channel-identity vs. content-validity.** *Captive-portal / MITM* is handled by **TLS cert-chain validation + hostname verification** — an impersonator can't present a cert that both chains to a trusted root *and* matches `database.lichess.org`, so the handshake fails → retry (in practice captive portals reset/blackhole HTTPS outright, caught earlier as a connect-timeout/stall). Note "valid chain to a root CA" is necessary but *not sufficient*: a portal can hold a valid cert for *its own* domain, so the identity pin is the **hostname** check — a distinct step that must not be disabled. TLS does **not** vouch for *content*, so a separate **content-sanity** layer (magic-byte sniff + parse-sanity, gates 5–6 below) guards against the *legitimate* endpoint — or a trusted intermediary — returning non-PGN over a flawless TLS session: CDN challenge / rate-limit / maintenance interstitials (HTML at 200/403/503), a wrong/stale/moved URL returning a 200 HTML index or soft-404 (CCRL's hand-curated mirrors especially), or a truncated/corrupt body. These are the *more likely* failure than MITM. **Root store: use the OS trust store** (`rustls-native-certs`) — natural on the macOS primary platform, and it keeps clawfish working on networks where the operator has legitimately installed a corporate root CA. The tradeoff — a *trusted*-MITM (corporate intercepting proxy) block page passes TLS — is absorbed by the content-sanity gates, which catch its HTML body. (Bundled Mozilla roots were the alternative: immune to an injected CA, but they fail *legitimately* on an intercepting corporate network → infinite backoff. Rejected.)
- **No `.partial` file on disk.** Since the resume mechanism is in-RAM and the corpus shard is the persistent state (already-ingested games dedup naturally on game_id), there's no need to checkpoint compressed bytes to disk. On full-process restart (e.g. machine reboot mid-fetch), we re-stream from byte 0 of the URL; the shard's dedup absorbs the already-ingested games as no-ops, and we continue past where we left off. CPU is wasted on the re-decompress + re-parse of already-ingested bytes, but the network bytes are re-downloaded only across full-process restarts — not across in-process retries.
- **URL catalog**: `docs/data-catalog.md` (new) vetting CCRL snapshot URLs + Lichess monthly URLs with their SHA-256s where known. Lichess URLs follow the predictable `https://database.lichess.org/standard/lichess_db_standard_rated_YYYY-MM.pgn.zst` pattern (driver can construct on demand); CCRL URLs are hand-curated (mirror selection, ratings-list-specific paths).
- **Per-URL state file** (`bench/corpus/fetch-state.json`) records which URLs have been touched, how many positions each contributed, and whether the stream terminated at EOS or was killed early. The driver consults this when deciding whether the same URL has more to give vs. moving to the next.
- **Integration with existing `corpus ingest-pgn`.** The new `corpus fetch --source={ccrl,lichess} --label <name> [--url <url>] --target-positions <N> --out <dir>` subcommand wires HTTP + decompress + parse into the existing `pgn-shard.bin` write path. `corpus ingest-pgn` continues to parse already-on-disk PGNs (for operator-staged sources or testing). Both feed the same shard log.
- **Gate.** M6.H is **data-acquisition infra**, like M6.G — no SPRT, no Elo claim. Landing gate is functional: a small CI / dev-loop test fetches a tiny pinned mirror and asserts the streaming + early-termination + resume behavior end-to-end. (No real Lichess/CCRL fetch in CI — that's an operator step.) Engine bench unchanged by construction (no `evaluate` touch).
- **Sequencing**: lands between M6.G (corpus infra) and M6.I (Texel pass). The M6.I bi-level driver calls M6.H's `corpus::fetch::stream_to_ingest(...)` whenever the learning-curve experiment needs more positions from CCRL or Lichess. M6.I treats M6.H as a synchronous "get me N more positions, block until done" primitive — exactly parallel to how it calls `corpus selfplay --opening-mode={book,random}` for self-play data.
- **Filter-spec amendment (2026-05-22, DECIDED pre-M6.I, not yet implemented).** The reused `filter::game_admitted` gains three game-level gates: drop `Termination "Rules infraction"` (joining `Time forfeit` / `Abandoned`); minimum game length `≥ 20 plies` (10 full moves — the `pgn-extract --minmoves 10` literature default; kills aborts / disconnects / mouse-slips, which cluster under ~5 moves); and a **universal non-standard-start gate** rejecting `[SetUp "1"]` / non-startpos `[FEN]` (covers CCRL Chess960/FRC, odds, and any from-position game across *all* sources — also a parser-correctness safeguard, since the parser replays from the standard start and must learn the `SetUp`+`FEN` tags). Recorded non-gates: **no** Elo-gap cap (the both-sides ≥2000 floor suffices; a gap is label *variance* not directional bias, absorbed by the per-candidate K refit, and a cap would over-represent drawish even-matchups), and **no** CCRL strength floor (`min_elo=2000` is already a no-op against engine-scale ratings). Resignations + draws-by-agreement are **kept** (good proxies under the floor / long-TC / min-length gates; agreement is undetectable from Lichess `Termination "Normal"` anyway). Full rationale: ADR-0036 amendment "(2026-05-22, game-level + variant filter spec)".

**Sub-protocol — robustness gates.** Each fetch attempt walks through these gates; failing any → log + backoff + retry (unless flagged "permanent" → nonzero exit / operator notification):

1. **Connect**: explicit connect timeout ~10s (ureq `Agent::set_connect_timeout`). TLS cert-chain validation **and hostname verification** enforced against the **OS trust store** (`rustls-native-certs`) — an untrusted captive-portal/MITM impersonating the host trips this and is a *good* signal (it's the hostname check, not the chain check alone, that pins identity).
2. **Stall watchdog**: stall ≝ *no new PGN game parsed for `T_stall` (≈60–90s)* — progress, not byte-liveness. `TcpStream::set_read_timeout` is set short (~10s) as a wake only (a `read()` timeout is not a failure — it just lets the thread re-check the watchdog + shutdown flag); the abort fires on `now − last_game_at > T_stall`, keyed on **games parsed** (not ingested positions — the filter is legitimately selective). Catches dead TCP *and* dribble / garbage-that-never-parses. Consecutive resume-then-immediate-restall escalates to restart-from-zero. No `--max-time`-equivalent — slow-but-progressing is fine.
3. **HTTP error — split by permanence**:
   - **Transient** (5xx, 408 Request Timeout, 429 Too Many Requests): backoff + retry.
   - **Permanent** (4xx excluding 408/429 — e.g. 404 Not Found, 410 Gone, 403 Forbidden): nonzero exit / operator notification. A misspelled URL or removed mirror must NOT infinite-loop the unattended overnight campaign.
4. **Redirect cap**: ureq follows redirects by default; cap at ~5 hops to defeat redirect loops. The content-sanity gates 5 + 6 (magic-byte sniff, parse-sanity) apply to the *final* response — a redirect landing on an HTML interstitial/login page (e.g. a trusted-MITM block page; an untrusted one already fails TLS at gate 1) still gets caught there.
5. **Magic-byte sniff** (content-sanity): first ~256 bytes of decompressed stream must look like PGN (`[Event `, `[White `, etc.). Catches the *legitimate* endpoint — or a trusted intermediary — returning HTML at 200 OK (CDN interstitial, soft-404, block page) after a decompression-soft-fail.
6. **Parse-sanity** (content-sanity): of the first ~100 games yielded by `stream_pgn`, `parse_failures / games_seen ≤ 0.1` (10% threshold — legit PGN streams parse cleanly; anything looser admits HTML that happened to contain a `[`-tag-looking byte sequence, or a truncated/corrupt body). On failure: discard + retry.
7. **Disk-space pre-check**: before opening the connection, `statvfs` the corpus directory and refuse if free space < safety margin (e.g. 2 GB or 3× expected compressed-file size, whichever is larger). A mid-fetch ENOSPC is recoverable per M6.G R1 (per-game CRC-framed append-block + atomic rename — `corpus fetch` reuses this discipline so partial games never make it to the shard), but the operator-surprise of a full disk on overnight crunch is avoidable cheaply.

**Operator dial.** When the campaign is left running unattended overnight and network goes down, the campaign waits — log lines like `corpus: fetch attempt 7 backing off until 03:42 local` make it clear what's happening on resume.

**M6.H2 scope detail — corpus lanes (flat build-ready lanes; build→finalize).**

- **Four flat independent lanes.** Each lane (`SelfPlayOnBook`, `SelfPlayOffBook`, `Ccrl`, `LichessOpen`) lives in its own `--out <dir>` and produces one flat `lane.bin` (the CRC-framed per-game-block `store.rs` format) + `manifest.json` + `filter_spec.txt` + `corpus_stats.txt`. The lanes are never co-located; each lane's `game_id` space is private (resume/dedup reads exactly one file).
- **Inline pipeline at generation.** All four lanes apply the same per-position pipeline (opening-skip `OPENING_SKIP_PLIES=8` → drop in-check → `|static_eval| ≤ HIGH_SCORE_CP=600` → `is_quiet` with `QUIET_MARGIN_CP=30`) then per-lane FEN dedup (first-seen-WITHIN-the-lane) → per-game reservoir cap (`PER_GAME_CAP=10`) → exact target truncation, routed through the shared `pipeline::LaneCommitter`. The committer's order matches the pre-M6.H2 consumer (dedup first, then cap among survivors, then exact target).
- **No split at generation.** The train/val split moves to M6.I, which reads the four flat lane dirs and owns the inter-lane mix + the split. `src/corpus/split.rs` deleted; `CorpusRecord::dedup_key` removed (dedup is first-seen-by-arrival within the lane, deterministic from the deterministic arrival stream).
- **No cross-lane dedup.** The same FEN may appear in up to four lanes, possibly with conflicting labels — explicitly accepted. ADR-0003's audit is per-lane (each lane's labels are original game outcomes) → still passes per lane.
- **Usable-count target.** `--target` (fetch) / `--cap-positions` (self-play) count USABLE positions (post quiet-filter → dedup → cap); the committer's exact truncation lands the lane on the target exactly. Per-lane target 2M usable → 8M total.

**M6.I scope detail.**

- **Texel as integration, not per-phase.** Per-phase parameter calibration (SPSA / hand-tune) is *permitted* but not mandated — each prior phase ships literature defaults. The M6.I joint Texel pass is where the cumulative tuning Elo is recovered. Expectation: +30–60 Elo across A–F from removing parameter-tuning slack on ~50–80 jointly-optimized weights.
- **Consumes the frozen M6.G corpus.** M6.I consumes the *frozen* M6.G corpus artifact + manifest (game-result-labeled per the M6.G ADR-0003 audit; no engine-score labels). The M6.G↔M6.I interface is fixed **interface-first** — the quiet-position definition + held-out set + stratified objective are pinned at M6.G plan time by the M6.I tuner contract.
- **Corpus-mixture meta-optimization (sub-protocol).** Bi-level: **inner Texel per candidate mixture**, **outer coarse simplex search** on the M6.G stratified held-out objective, **K refit per candidate** (K is not shared across candidates), **SPRT only to confirm the chosen winner** — never inside the search loop (the search loop uses the held-out objective, not SPRT). The chosen mixture + its sensitivity is reported alongside the per-parameter sensitivity table.
- **Optimizer stopping criterion (sub-protocol).** Do **not** stop on training loss (that drives the proxy — logistic loss ≠ Elo — to its minimum: proxy-overfit, worst for the ~970-param Arm-B co-tune). Stop on the **stratified held-out objective**: early-stop with patience, tolerance δ at the validation-metric noise floor, restore the *best* checkpoint (not the last iterate); do not stop on the aggregate while the blind-spot strata (outposts, endgame) still improve. Plus a domain stop — the **integer-centipawn quantization floor**: once the integer-rounded weight vector stops changing across a window, further loss reduction has zero deployment effect (pure proxy-overfit below engine resolution). **Gauge-aware:** if K is co-optimized the K·scale-invariant direction is a flat ridge — measure convergence only in the *identified* subspace and pin the gauge (frozen-PST anchor / fixed K-refit schedule); table-indexed / clamp / gate params have ≈zero gradient a.e., so Adam tunes the linear value-weights and structural params are swept separately, not stopped on. **The real stop is the SPRT, not the optimizer:** inner early-stop yields *candidates* (keep a few near the val optimum — val-loss-optimal ≠ Elo-optimal); the verdict ladder decides, and verdict-4 ⇒ revert, not "optimize more." Composite "inner exhausted" = val plateau within noise ∧ integer-rounded weights stable ∧ small update-norm in the identified subspace, with a **deterministic max-iter / wall-clock backstop** (non-convergence within the cap is itself a reported diagnostic). All stopping constants (seed, patience, δ, max-iter, eval cadence, K-refit cadence, restore-best) are recorded in the params manifest — the reproducibility mandate forbids a "looks converged" judgment call. **Arm-B note:** with the ~970 ill-conditioned co-tune, early stopping is itself a regularizer interacting with the ridge λ — keep it conservative and nest it in the λ-sweep, not tuned independently (see the tuning-backlog "M6.I PST co-tuning" entry). The stopping *philosophy* is optimizer-agnostic; only the gauge / zero-gradient diagnostics are Adam-specific — the M6.I optimizer choice remains an open ADR (Texel local-search vs SPSA vs gradient/Adam).
- **Reproducibility and per-parameter sensitivity.** Harness outputs `bench/m6-params.json` + a re-run script that reproduces the tuned vector from the frozen M6.G corpus + RNG seed. **In all outcomes** (H1-accept, small-positive, regression, inconclusive) the harness records a per-parameter sensitivity table — for each tunable, the loss-function derivative at the optimization endpoint and the SPRT-margin sensitivity over a ±10% perturbation — plus the chosen mixture and its sensitivity. This is load-bearing for diagnosis when M6.I's SPRT verdict is *not* clean accept.
- **Verdict ladder for the joint-tune SPRT vs. `M6.F`.** M6.I has *more* surface than M5.H2/M5.I and a different "fail" mode (the tuned vector may be small-positive but indistinguishable from literature, which is real Elo at risk if reverted). Verdict ladder:
  1. **H1-accept** (CI lower ≥ 5): ship tuned, tag `M6.I` at tuned build.
  2. **Small-but-not-regression** (mean ≥ 5 ∧ CI lower > −10) — matches M5.F's plan §11 outcome 2: ship tuned, tag `M6.I` at tuned build, retrospective notes the inconclusive SPRT.
  3. **Marginal-positive** (mean ≥ 0 ∧ CI lower > −10) — *a new rung specific to M6.I's joint-tune failure mode, not from M5.F precedent.* Ship tuned with retrospective caveat. Rationale: Texel's joint retune of 50–80 parameters can land at small-positive mean even when each individual parameter moved correctly — reverting would discard real gain. Outcome 3 lives between M5.F-style outcome 2 and the regression rung.
  4. **Regression or wide-CI negative** (mean < 0 ∨ CI lower ≤ −10): **revert** to literature defaults — M6 ships at `M6.F`, the `M6.I` tag is placed at `M6.F`'s commit, retrospective documents the failed Texel attempt and recommends a follow-up SPSA round on the literature-default parameters (mid-milestone tune precedent: M5.G v2 retune). M5.H2/M5.I deferral pattern applies only at outcome 4.

**Operational robustness (binding on the M6.G + M6.I plans/ADRs).** Both the M6.G self-play corpus generator and the M6.I tuner are long heavy-crunch jobs run on a *laptop* dev environment — carried around, OS-suspended (lid-close), occasionally offline, and `renice`d to background while the machine is in interactive use. The following are hard requirements, not nice-to-haves; the interrupted-game-exclusion clause is a **correctness gate** (M5.E "correctness over Elo" precedent) and feeds the M6.G data-quality gate.

- **R1 — crash-safe durability (must survive `SIGKILL` / power-loss / OOM-kill / panic, not only graceful exit).** Incremental atomic persistence: write-temp → `fsync` → atomic rename; a checkpoint is never observable half-written. Max work lost on a hard kill = one in-flight unit (one half-finished self-play game; one optimizer step / mini-batch). **Load-bearing:** correctness must NOT depend on a signal handler running — power loss runs none. The graceful path (R7) only *additionally* saves the in-flight game; the guarantee comes from R1's atomic incremental writes alone.
- **R2 — interrupted games cleanly excluded (correctness gate).** An incomplete game contributes **zero** labels — not scored as a loss for the side to move, not a draw, not adjudicated: dropped entirely. **Mechanism:** positions are committed *transactionally* — buffered against their game and flushed only **after** the game reaches a natural terminal result; never flushed before the outcome exists. A spurious label from an interrupted game is exactly the biased label-noise that poisons Texel.
- **R3 — resumable, deterministic across interruption.** Restart regenerates nothing completed; the tuner resumes from checkpointed {params, best-params, Adam/optimizer moments, iter count, RNG state, K, early-stop/patience counters, validation history} so the resumed run is **bit-identical** to an uninterrupted run modulo the one lost in-flight unit. The roadmap's seed + re-run reproducibility mandate must hold *across* interruption.
- **R4 — renice + OS-suspend handled by an asset already shipped (ADR-0021 `VirtualClock`).** `VirtualClock` (CPU-thread-time via `CLOCK_THREAD_CPUTIME_ID`, ELOH.C) already makes clawfish-vs-clawfish play load-independent: `renice` → same CPU budget → ≈ equal achieved depth, only ≈2× wall clock; and a thread accrues **zero** CPU time during OS suspend, so a self-play game spanning a lid-close is **not mis-timed and is NOT discarded on timing grounds**. **Therefore:** mandate `VirtualClock` for M6.G self-play *and* the M6.I clawfish-vs-`M6.F` SPRT; the residual new-code burden is only the process-death path (R1/R2). **Precondition:** the ADR-0021 operator `CLOCK_THREAD_CPUTIME_ID` probe must pass on the dev machine (the M4/macOS-26 machine already passed, 0.9993). **Forward flag:** `VirtualClock` is per-*thread*; search is single-threaded today (each parallel self-play game its own correct clock) — revisit the semantics if Lazy SMP lands.
- **R5 — no hard network dependency during crunch.** Originally framed as: corpus sources (CCRL / Lichess) are staged to local vendored data *before* the long phase; self-play and tuning run fully offline. **Amended by M6.H** (Zurichess rejected per ADR-0003): on-demand `corpus fetch` is now permitted *during* the campaign because M6.H's outer infinite-backoff loop blocks the campaign on network failure rather than crashing it — spotty connectivity stalls but cannot fail a run, satisfying R5 in spirit. Pre-staging via `corpus ingest-pgn` against already-on-disk PGNs remains a valid alternative path. Self-play campaigns are fully offline by construction.
- **R6 — bounded memory (laptop, not a 64 GB server).** Configurable modest peak; corpus streamed to/from disk with bounded buffers — peak RAM must not scale with the multi-million-position corpus size. Documented ceiling + config knob.
- **R7 — all cores, renice-friendly.** Uses all cores by default (configurable worker count); degrades cleanly under `nice`/low priority — no busy-wait spinlocks, no priority inversion where a deprioritized worker holds a lock that blocks checkpointing. Graceful on `SIGTERM`/`SIGINT` (drop-or-finish the in-flight unit, flush a clean checkpoint, exit); *safe* on `SIGKILL`/power-loss via R1 alone.
- **R-TC — self-play TC is moderate + deployment-mirroring, not long.** Corpus quality is dominated by *decorrelated volume* within the laptop CPU budget, and clawfish's eval has demonstrated TC-dependent behavior (the M6.B–D slow-TC reversals), so self-play uses a **moderate, TC-stratified profile mirroring the deployment mixed-TC SPRT buckets** (or fixed-shallow-depth stratified across their effective depths) — *not* a single long TC (sharply diminishing label-fidelity per huge cost; mismatches the mixed-TC deployment objective) and *not* bullet-equivalent (clock-blunder label noise). TC is another stratification axis of the M6.G corpus, folding into the stratified mixture objective. (ADR-0021 §4's node-TC rejection is scoped to *cross-version SPRT comparability* and does NOT forbid fixed-depth/fixed-nodes for *corpus generation* — one fixed build, not a version comparison.)
- **Verification (the requirement is not done until this passes — part of the M6.G/M6.I plan test-suite).** `kill -9` at randomized points + a simulated suspend → assert (a) the corpus contains **zero** partial-game labels and game count equals completed-before-kill, (b) the tuner resumes to a state bit-identical to the no-interruption run.

**Sequencing rationale.** A first because every downstream phase needs MG/EG separation in the eval representation. B before C because C reads `passed_pawns[color]` from B's pawn-hash cache (real code dependency). E after B for the same reason (king safety reads `pawn_shield_files[color]` from B's pawn hash). E after D is *not* a code dependency (attack bitboards are already in tree from M1) but an SPRT-signal-attribution choice — landing mobility before king-safety means the mobility-area / king-zone interaction is captured in M6.E's signal, not M6.D's. F after E is a real code dependency (outpost / rook-on-file reuse M6.B's pawn-attack-span fills + the `file_fill` helper). G (corpus) is data infra largely independent of the M6.F eval features — it can proceed in parallel with M6.F, but its quiet-position definition + held-out set + stratified objective are pinned by the M6.I tuner contract at M6.G plan time (interface-first). H (robust on-demand Lichess/CCRL ingestion) is a network-layer extension of M6.G's data infra — it lands after G (depends on G's `pgn-shard.bin` write path + the `Source` taxonomy) but before I (M6.I's bi-level driver invokes M6.H's fetch as a "give me N more positions" primitive). I (Texel) last because it needs *both* the parameter surface fully expanded across A–F (the "extend then tune" law — Texel cannot discover absent features, so the M6.F Tier-1 features must exist before the one joint pass) *and* the M6.G corpus + the M6.H fetcher it consumes during the learning-curve experiment.

**Parallelization opportunities.**

- **Research and planning fan out freely.** SPRT gates are inherently sequential (each phase needs a stable predecessor baseline tag, and mixed-TC SPRT wallclock is ~10 hours per phase per the M5 precedent), but the upstream `research → plan → tests` work for downstream phases can run concurrently with earlier phases' implementation and SPRT runs. Concretely: while M6.A is in SPRT, kick off M6.B research + plan-mode in parallel.
- **Implementation parallelism is constrained, not "free."** M6.D (mobility), M6.G (corpus data infra), and M6.I (Texel binary) have the smallest code overlap with A–C — M6.D adds new helpers to `eval.rs`'s evaluator, M6.G is a standalone data pipeline + vendored corpus artifact (no `evaluate(&Position)` touch at all), M6.I lives in its own `src/bin/texel-tune.rs` binary. M6.G's acquisition/filtering work is largely independent of the M6.F eval features and can run **in parallel with M6.F** (only its quiet-definition / held-out / objective interface is pinned by the M6.I tuner contract at M6.G plan time). But every M6.A–F phase merges through the same `evaluate(&Position)` integration point, so a long-lived feature branch for M6.D will incur a non-trivial rebase tax as M6.A's `(mg, eg, phase)` representation, M6.B's pawn-info struct, and M6.C's passed-pawn-reads all land on `main`. **Implementation should default to rebasing atop each preceding phase, not branching off pre-A.** Research and tests for M6.D can be drafted in parallel with M6.B/C work, but the implementation merge is sequential.
- **Phases with code-region overlap must sequence:**
  - **A, B, C** all extend the eval evaluator and (for B/C) the pawn-info path.
  - **E** reads pawn-shield from B's pawn-info — real code dependency on B.
  - **F** (outpost / rook-on-file) reads B's pawn-attack-span fills + the `file_fill` helper — real code dependency on B (lands after E in the chain).
- **Practical recommendation:** process the eval-SPRT chain serially A → B → C → D → E → F to keep Elo attribution clean and minimise merge churn against `evaluate(&Position)`; G (corpus) runs in parallel with F (data infra, no SPRT, no `evaluate` touch); H (network-layer fetcher, no `evaluate` touch) lands after G; and I (Texel) follows once F, G, and H are in. Plan and write tests for D, F, G, H, and I concurrently with earlier phases' SPRT runs so the agent isn't idle during long SPRT wallclock.

**M6.K scope detail — king-safety attacker S-curve activation (planned).**

- **Why this is its own phase and not a tuning-backlog item anymore.** The directly-measured M6.J STS King Activity 555 / AKPC 628 results identify king attackers as the **largest remaining classical-eval gap**. The fix structurally cannot be done by M6.I/M6.J's Texel-on-quiet-positions pipeline — the S-curve is non-linear in the tunable surface (the attacker-count index `units = Σ multiplier_kind · attackers` makes the term non-linear in the multipliers; with multipliers shipped at zero ⇒ `units ≡ 0` ⇒ only `KING_SAFETY_TABLE[0]` reachable ⇒ table dead by construction) AND the corpus's quiet filter strips exactly the sharp king-attack positions whose game outcome would train it. King danger is realized in **games**, so tune on games (SPSA + SPRT), not on a quiet-position static regression. This is the standard engine practice (`docs/research/m6-texel-tuning.md` §4 — freeze the non-linear params and sweep them outside the linear core). Promoting it from `docs/tuning-backlog.md` item 9 into a milestone signals operator commitment and a planned campaign cadence; the tuning-backlog item is superseded by this row.
- **Two-stage methodology — cheap probe first, deflate only if needed.** Stage 1 ("literature on/off") is one mixed-TC + virtual-clock SPRT vs `M6.J` with the literature CPW S-curve at full magnitude (multipliers 2/2/3/4, table from literature, global gain `g=1`). If H1, ship — the M6.E HIGH transfer-risk worry (PeSTO MG king PST already prices ~30–50 cp of castled-king safety ⇒ literature term is a double-count) didn't bite at the *joint-tuned* M6.J landscape. If H0 or regress, the double-count is real and stage 2 fires. Stage 2 ("SPSA-deflate") tunes the global gain `g ∈ (0, 1]` (the deflation lever) and, if needed, the 4 per-kind multipliers, by SPSA against SPRT. A manual coordinate grid `g ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0}` × a coarse multiplier sweep is an acceptable substitute if the SPSA harness landing is deferred. The grid is small (~5 SPRT-equivalent campaigns).
- **What NOT to tune.** Do *not* tune the 100 individual `KING_SAFETY_TABLE` entries directly — no signal at the corpus scale, guaranteed overfit. Keep the literature CPW S-curve *shape*; the deflation is the lever. The optional gate thresholds (`<2 attackers ∨ no queen`) stay fixed unless stage 2 is marginal and the gate proves load-bearing.
- **Tunable surface.** 4 attacker multipliers (`KING_ATTACK_WEIGHT_{N,B,R,Q}`) + 1 global gain `g`. Stage 1 fixes all five at literature defaults (`g=1`). Stage 2 sweeps `g` first (highest leverage), adds the multipliers only if `g`-alone is insufficient.

### M7 — SEE (static exchange evaluation) + the ordering/pruning it unblocks
Static Exchange Evaluation: resolve a capture sequence on a target square to its material outcome (least-valuable-attacker recapture order, with x-ray attackers behind the first capturer, promotion-on-capture, and en passant). SEE is a **search** primitive — it needs only piece values — so, unlike the hand-crafted eval terms M6 tuned, **NNUE does not obsolete it**: SEE-based capture ordering and qsearch pruning persist under a learned eval. It is therefore a *permanent* investment, slotted here (right after the classical eval core, before NNUE) so it is reaped across every later milestone, and because it unblocks a cluster of already-specified deferrals (ADR-0016 SEE capture filter, ADR-0026 SEE capture-futility / delta pruning, ADR-0030 good/bad capture split). Inserted 2026-05-25 (renumbering the former M7–M13 → M8–M14).

Decomposed into SPRT-gated sub-phases (the M5 per-technique precedent):

- **M7.A — SEE evaluator + capture-ordering split.** The exchange resolver + split captures into good (SEE ≥ 0) / bad (SEE < 0): good captures stay ahead of killers/quiets, bad captures deferred after them (ADR-0030 stage-2/6 split). **The foundation — the minimum viable SEE landing; everything else consumes it.** Impl is small (~150–250 LOC resolver + ~50–100 LOC stager split) but **verification-heavy** (curated exchange positions covering x-ray / promotion / en-passant + a property test vs a slow reference resolver — more test LOC than impl).
- **M7.B — qsearch SEE-pruning.** Skip SEE < 0 captures in quiescence. Small; own SPRT.
- **M7.C — SEE capture futility / delta pruning.** SEE-gated futility margins at frontier nodes (the ADR-0026 extension). Small; own SPRT.

**M7.A is a hard prerequisite for M7.B / M7.C** (shared evaluator), so they land in order; .B/.C are independently SPRT-able fast-follows. Side benefit: better capture ordering yields faster cutoffs → fewer nodes per depth at *every* TC (a partial offset to the M6.I richer-eval node-cost, and a help at fast TC specifically).

**Exit criteria.**
- **SEE correctness:** the evaluator matches hand-computed exchange outcomes on a curated suite (x-ray stacks, promotion captures, en passant, multi-defender squares) + a property test vs a slow reference resolver over random positions.
- **SPRT:** each sub-phase mixed-TC SPRT vs the prior tag (M7.A vs `M6.I`) per the standard verdict ladder; marginal-but-positive lands per the M5.F precedent, a clear regression reverts that sub-phase.

**Schedulable note.** No hard dependency beyond M1 (movegen / attacks) + the existing M3–M5 search; feasible at HEAD. Placed first among the post-M6 milestones because it is the only *permanent strength-depth* gain in the M7–M11 window (opening books / tablebases are data breadth, the skill dial is strength-*reduction*, parallelism multiplies whatever search exists). ADR allocated at landing.

### M8 — Opening books + endgame tablebases
Two data-driven integrations, both gated by ADR-0003 (no engine source, but general-purpose libraries and public chess data are fine — `CLAUDE.md` "Domain code restrictions"). Each is sub-milestone-sized; the milestone lands as a pair of sub-phases.

- **M8.A — Opening books (Polyglot).** Binary `.bin` decoder over sorted `(zobrist u64, move u16, weight u16, learn u32)` records, big-endian, binary search. Polyglot move encoding → our internal `Move` (incl. castling = king-takes-own-rook). Weighted-random vs best-weight selection policy. UCI options (`OwnBook`, `BookFile`, optional `BookDepth` / `BookSelectivity`). Root probe before search; on miss, fall through to the regular search driver. The M1.D Polyglot Zobrist key set (ADR-0009) makes any published Polyglot book probable bit-identically — this milestone is pure-add. ~500–800 LOC + one ADR.
- **M8.B — Endgame tablebases (Syzygy).** WDL probe in-tree → exact-bound TT store; DTZ probe at the root → rule-50-safe best-move selection. UCI options (`SyzygyPath`, `SyzygyProbeDepth`, `SyzygyProbeLimit`, `Syzygy50MoveRule`). **Crate-vs-in-house is an open ADR-0003 question, not a deferred maybe.** The candidate crate (`shakmaty-syzygy` and any equivalent) depends transitively on `shakmaty`, which is a full move generator + position type — i.e. a chess-engine kernel under ADR-0003's definition. Even using the Syzygy crate "as a binary-format library" pulls a chess engine in as a transitive dependency, and the probe path legitimately needs to apply moves for canonicalization (DTZ best-move selection in particular). So either (i) we accept the transitive dependency and amend ADR-0003 to permit chess-engine *libraries that don't influence our engine's algorithms* (data-decoder framing), or (ii) we commit upfront to an in-house decoder, budgeting ~2000–3000 LOC of intricate format work (canonical position indexing with symmetry reductions, Huffman/RE-Pair decompression, per-material-config table dispatch). The M8.B plan/ADR resolves this *before* implementation starts. Rule-50 / GHI interaction touches the same ground as M4.A's TT-aware halfmove story (`decisions/0018` GHI clause).

**Sub-phase independence.** M8.A and M8.B may close independently — each lands its own baseline tag and its own retrospective. The Polyglot half is genuinely small-and-safe (vendored Zobrist already in tree, binary search over a sorted file); the Syzygy half is substantially larger and gated by the ADR-0003 question above. Bundling them under a single M8 close would let the easy half wait on the hard half, which is the wrong incentive. The milestone *as a whole* closes when both halves have landed.

**Exit criteria.**
- **M8.A correctness:** book play verified by hash-compat round-trip from M1.D's vendored Polyglot key set against a published `.bin` (e.g. `book_small.bin` or comparable). Weight-decode round-trip from raw bytes verified against published reference values.
- **M8.B correctness:** WDL probe matches Stockfish-as-oracle (per ADR-0006) on a generated EPD corpus of 3-/4-/5-piece positions (target: ≥1000 positions sampled across material configs). DTZ probe matches Stockfish best-move selection at the root for a smaller curated set including the well-known KBNK-vs-mate-in-N and rule-50-near-boundary positions.
- **Gating:** both features disabled by default at engine startup, enabled per UCI option.
- **SPRT:** at M5/M6 typical TCs and SPRT opening-book regimes, the Elo signal of either half is small-but-not-zero. The harness supplies openings during SPRT (diluting M8.A's contribution to ~0 by design); tablebase probes do affect a non-trivial fraction of decisive long-game endings (KPK races, KRP endings, KQK proof) and *can* flip outcomes, but the per-game frequency at SPRT TCs is modest. We therefore make correctness the M8.A/M8.B landing gate (per the M5.E precedent for correctness-only milestones) and defer the Elo-confirmation regime to M13 (tournament play vs external opponents at long TC), where both features pay off most. Optional defensive SPRT against `M6.<close>` is permitted but not gating.

**Schedulable earlier.** Opening books require only M2 (UCI) + M1.D (Polyglot Zobrist) — feasible at HEAD. Tablebases require M1 (legal movegen) + M4 (TT integration) modulo the ADR-0003 question — also feasible at HEAD, contingent on the ADR resolving in favor of either path. Either or both could be pulled forward as sidequests during M6 if a low-energy slot opens. Optimal landing is here, where the engine has stable post-M6 eval and the integration is in place well before NNUE (M11) and tournament play (M13).

### M9 — Skill dial (basic strength reduction)
Configurable strength reduction: UCI's standard `UCI_LimitStrength` and `UCI_Elo` options, plus a granular "skill level" knob. Mechanisms: depth/node-count caps, eval noise injection, top-N randomized move selection. Each mechanism is a pure function (TDD-able); their composition's actual Elo at each setting is calibrated empirically via self-play matches.

**Exit criteria:** at advertised Elo settings, calibrated self-play matches confirm strength within a stated margin (e.g. ±50 Elo). Engine plays "interestingly bad" at low settings rather than randomly bad.

**Schedulable earlier.** This milestone has no hard dependency past M3 — if the user wants to play against the engine before M9, we can pull a basic version forward. Full calibration only makes sense once eval is reasonably stable (post-M6).

See `decisions/0005-strength-dial-as-planned-milestone.md`.

### M10 — Parallelism
Lazy SMP or equivalent. Lockless TT.

### M11 — NNUE
Train and integrate. Requires a data pipeline and training infrastructure separate from the engine. Replaces classical eval as the primary scoring function. Re-uses make/unmake hooks from `decisions/0004`.

### M12 — Android app
Wrap engine as a mobile app. Toy target — performance regressions vs. macOS expected and acceptable. The skill dial (M9) is the primary mechanism for making the mobile engine a plausible opponent for the user.

### M13 — Tournament play
Enter CCRL Blitz, CEGT, or open TalkChess events to obtain external Elo.

### M14 — Human-like play (optional, post-NNUE)
A separate model (Maia-style — trained to predict human moves at a target rating band) plugged in via the same eval/policy hook used by NNUE. Distinct from M9's "skill dial," which is an engine playing weakly; this is an engine playing *like a human* of a target rating. Open question: whether worth the training infrastructure investment, given M9 may be sufficient for the app use case.

## Long-term strength target

GM-level (~2700+) is the design ceiling. Classical eval (M3–M6) targets the high-amateur to weak-master range; NNUE (M11) is what carries us into GM territory. The skill dial (M9) and optional human-like play (M14) provide the inverse — making the engine a calibrated opponent at lower levels.
